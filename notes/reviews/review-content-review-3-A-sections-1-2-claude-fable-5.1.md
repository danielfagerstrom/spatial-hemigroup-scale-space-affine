# Referee report, Part A — block §§ 1–2 (pp. 1–14), the rest read for consistency

Manuscript: *Spatial scale space from hemigroup axioms: affine covariance and shape adaptation* (working draft, 110 pp.). I reviewed §§ 1–2 in detail (the abstract, the introduction with its paragraphs "What is an affine scale space?" to "Smaller groups", "Scope", the two "Relation to" paragraphs, "Plan", § 1.1, and § 2 with the cited facts C1–C4 and Table 1), and read §§ 3–5 and Appendices A, C.1 and I for consistency with them. Page numbers are those of the PDF. I checked every formula of § 2 by hand, rendered pp. 3, 9–14 to read the typeset mathematics where the text layer is ambiguous, and searched the published literature where a citation in §§ 1–2 is load-bearing; several source sites (Springer, the Saarland server) are not reachable from this session, so some page-level citations remain unverified and are marked as such.

## 1. Overall assessment and recommendation

For the block §§ 1–2: **minor revision**. The mathematics restated in § 2 is correct as far as I can check it: the admissible-exponent integrability conditions (2.1), the polar normalization (2.4)–(2.5), the subordination formula (2.6) (which I re-derived from the Gaussian-mixture Lévy measure), the conventions a = 2a_V and k = k_d/|S^{d-1}| (half the folded profile at d = 1), the constants of Lemma 2.9 (the factor c/2 in clause (2) agrees with κ = ½|S^{d-1}|k(0+) in Proposition 4.19, p. 46, and with k_d(0+) = 2γ for the Matérn law of index γ), the uniqueness clause of Theorem 2.5, and the four cited facts C1–C4 are true statements correctly transcribed from standard sources. The weaknesses of the block are in the headline prose, not in the mathematics: the abstract and § 1 present Table 2 as "the classification" when only two of its nine cells are complete descriptions and the text says so only in § 5 (p. 54); two imaging sentences of § 1 lack qualifications that the body makes; one scope sentence ("off W a change of aperture is a re-measurement", p. 2) states as fact what the axioms merely leave unrequired; § 1.1's account of the trust base leaves a reader unable to tell what status the line paper's statements have in the formal development; and one historical attribution (Iijima's matrix-scale axiomatic, p. 5) could not be confirmed from the published abstract of the source and should be quoted. The introduction is also about twice as long as a journal introduction should be, because it duplicates § 3.5 and the "Prior art" paragraphs of § 4. None of this touches the theorems; all of it is fixable without new mathematics.

## 2. The headline claims against what is proved

**"The main result describes the families of three groups ... completely in some cases, by bounds or examples in others" (abstract, p. 1); "The main result of the paper is the classification of the families in dimension d ≥ 2 over the two choices, Table 2" (p. 3).** This is stronger than what Table 2 (p. 32) carries. Reading the cells against their cited statements: (GL(d), rays) is complete (Propositions 4.5 and 3.4(3)); (GL(d), Loewner) is complete (Theorem 3.6); (GL(d), stable) is complete only for the stable orbits and gives a sufficient condition for general profiles (the converse is open problem 1); the three similarity cells and the three diagonal cells are bounds, examples, or descriptions on part of the parameters. § 5 (p. 54) says this honestly — "four cells of Table 2 stop short of a classification ... Three more are classified on part of their parameters" — which is seven of nine. The abstract's "completely in some cases" and § 1's "a complete description in some cases" (p. 3) are literally true but leave the wrong impression, and the text does not say cell by cell which are complete until § 5. Table 2 itself carries no mark of completeness. Required: say in the abstract and on p. 3 that two cells are complete (and which), and mark each cell of Table 2, or each row of the "inclusions" list on p. 31, as complete or partial. Calling a summary table "the main result" is also odd when the theorems that do the work are 3.6/3.7 and 4.21; I would name those as the main results and the table as the map.

**One rank-deficient step forces the Gaussian (Theorems 3.6, 3.7, 3.12; abstract and p. 4).** Consistent. The abstract's "an order with one step from a positive definite matrix that widens the aperture in some directions and leaves it unchanged in others admits only Gaussian families" is Proposition 4.22(3) (p. 49), whose hypothesis (a GL(d)-invariant order of the scheme containing w I ≤_W w M with λ_min(M) = 1, M ≠ I) covers every rank-deficient step from a positive definite base after transport, as p. 4 explains. The restriction to d ≥ 2 and to a positive definite base is stated on p. 4 ("The base must be positive definite"). No overclaim here.

**Shape-adapted apertures are such steps (abstract; p. 4; Proposition 4.22(1)).** The body is careful: the step is rank-deficient under the normalization of Lindeberg and Gårding that fixes the least eigenvalue (p. 4, p. 49), and under the fixed-volume normalization of Baumberg there is no Loewner step at all, for the Gaussian either (§ 4.1 item 1, p. 27; Lemma 4.1(2)). The abstract drops the normalization: "Shape-adapted apertures are such steps from the first stage" is unqualified. Required: add "under the normalization of Lindeberg and Gårding" (or "at fixed least smoothing") in the abstract. I could not reach the Lindeberg–Gårding texts to confirm the least-eigenvalue normalization and the rejection of fixed determinant "because it overestimates the anisotropy" ([37], § 6.1, cited p. 27); the description is specific enough to be credible, but since Proposition 4.22 and the abstract rest on it, the author should quote the sentence of [37] § 6.1 or [36] p. 396 in the paper.

**"Every other such kernel measures the image afresh there, as the iteration of shape adaptation does anyway" (abstract) and "a non-Gaussian kernel loses only the cascade between the stages of the iteration, which the iteration does not use (§ 5)" (p. 4).** The second is stronger than § 5, which says "as far as the apertures are concerned, a non-Gaussian kernel loses nothing the practice uses" and lists as a limitation that "how the selection and its iteration behave for the non-Gaussian orbits is not treated" (p. 54). A non-Gaussian first stage changes the second-moment matrix that selects the shape, so "loses only" is not established. Required: carry § 5's qualification into p. 4.

**"Off W a change of aperture is a re-measurement of the original image" (p. 2, end of the second bullet).** Off W the axioms require nothing; a step outside the chosen order may still have a probability increment (the stable orbit cascades on the full-rank steps of its cone, p. 39, which lie outside the ray order). Required: "need not be a further smoothing".

**The speed limit (Theorem 4.21, Table 3; abstract and p. 4).** Consistent with the theorem as stated (p. 47–48): γ ≥ α exactly, and F_α Gaussian for 2 ≤ α ≤ ∞. One nuance: p. 4 says "Table 3 gives the limit kernel by kernel", but Table 3 (p. 51) gives "at least" for tempered and general profiles and "not known in general"; "kernel by kernel" oversells by a little. Suggest "for each family of the table".

**Rays only (Propositions 4.15, 4.17, 4.18, 4.19).** Consistent; p. 4's "orbits whose transform decays like a power of the frequency keep at most the rays" is the hypothesis of Proposition 4.15, and "at most" is right since which rays carry positive increments is a separate question (p. 44).

**Smaller groups and "the rigidity is a property of the group".** p. 5: "the similarity row keeps every rotation-invariant one in every column, the Loewner column included (Proposition F.3(4))". I checked that this holds: the shape-blind family Σ ↦ ((det Σ)^{1/2d} I)_* m (p. 61) is covariant under the similarities, continuous on PSD(d), and cascades along every Loewner step because the volume scale is monotone along the Loewner order (Lemma 4.1(2)) and m is self-decomposable. But two things should be said: Table 2's (similarities, Loewner) cell (p. 32) does not state this fact at all, so a reader of the table cannot find it; and the witnessing family is the degenerate one whose kernels at every singular Σ are δ_0 (p. 61), so "keeps every rotation-invariant law" is kept by a family that sees only the volume of the aperture. p. 5's reading "the Loewner order alone does not force the Gaussian" is nonetheless correct (and Proposition H.1(3) gives a non-degenerate witness). The sentence "the general linear group alone does not either (Theorem 4.21(3))" is correct.

**Scope sentences (p. 5 and § 5).** "The paper is theory and makes no empirical claim about images" is right and welcome. "The isotropic theory in R^d is that of the isotropic paper, used through the six statements restated in § 2.3" undercounts: four more statements of [16] are cited inline (Props. 3.11, 3.12(2), Thm. 3.13 on p. 46, Rem. 3.26 on p. 26); say "restated six and cited four more".

## 3. Mathematical correctness (§ 2, and the uses of § 2 elsewhere)

I found no error in § 2. Details checked:

- (2.1), p. 9: the folded Lévy measure k(x) dx/x needs ∫_0^1 x k(x) dx < ∞ and ∫_1^∞ k(x) dx/x < ∞; correct, and the identification with symmetric self-decomposable laws (k nonincreasing) is Sato's Cor. 15.11, consistently cited through [15].
- Theorem 2.3's uniqueness and Theorem 2.5's uniqueness clause (p. 10): the clause allows any real F′ with F′(0) = 0. It holds because F ≢ 0 self-decomposable exponents are nondecreasing (|μ̂(ξ)| ≤ |μ̂(cξ)| from (2.2)), so F(ar) = F(br) for all r forces a = b. Fine, but a one-clause reason would help the reader who wonders why F′ is not required to lie in A_d.
- Definition 2.8, p. 11: with f_d(r) = k_d(r)/(|S^{d-1}| r^d) the polar formula (2.4) and the integrability conditions follow; at d = 1, k_d is the folded profile and k = k_d/2, as the list on p. 14 says. The relation a = 2a_V (p. 13) is right.
- (2.6), p. 11: I re-derived it. The Lévy density of a Gaussian variance mixture with delay profile k_I is ∫_0^∞ (2πu)^{-d/2} e^{-r²/2u} k_I(u) du/u; substituting v = r²/2u and multiplying by |S^{d-1}| r^d gives (2/Γ(d/2)) ∫ v^{d/2-1} e^{-v} k_I(r²/2v) dv. Correct, and k_I nonincreasing makes k_d nonincreasing. The Matérn example (p. 11: Laplace transform (1 + t²σ)^{-γ}) matches Proposition 4.4(4) (p. 36).
- Lemma 2.9, p. 12: clause (1)'s constant 13c/4 is generous (the sharp constant of the r < 1 part is about 0.6c) but valid; clause (2)'s "c/2 log a" for g(u) = F_d(√u e) is right and is what Proposition 4.19 uses (κ = c/2). (The pdftotext layer prints this fraction as "2c"; the typeset page is correct.)
- C1 (p. 12): matches Sato's Theorem 9.3 as secondary sources quote it (null array, centred row sums, limit infinitely divisible); the restatement for laws is harmless.
- C2 (p. 12): Sato's Theorem 8.1 verbatim.
- C3 (p. 13): the mathematics is right. The paragraph justifying the step from "each residual of one family" to "every probability measure that is a residual at some t ≥ 0" ("their sentence applied to a family completed around that measure") is obscure. The clean reason: once μ is infinitely divisible its transform has no zero, so the residual at each t is the unique measure with transform μ̂/μ̂(e^{-tBᵀ}·). State that, or state C3 for a family. Also note that t = 0 is included; then ρ_0 = δ_0 by the same uniqueness, so nothing is lost.
- C4 (p. 13): true (it is the Horn/Gel'fand–Naimark inequality for the product of positive definite matrices, λ(MN) = λ(M^{1/2}NM^{1/2})), and the use in Lemma C.1(2) (p. 72) subtracts from the equal totals correctly. I could not verify that Bhatia's Cor. III.4.6 is numbered and paged as cited; a secondary source cites that corollary for a weak-majorization statement about λ(√A B √A), which is the same inequality in another form.
- Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2, Definition 2.4, Lemmas 2.6–2.7: correctly stated; Lemma A.1(5)/A.2(3) (pp. 57–60) do bridge the operator form (A7, L¹-continuity) and the kernel form (I2, pointwise transforms) as § 2.3 promises.

Two small things. "A gauge is an increasing bijection χ of [0, ∞), hence continuous, with χ(0) = 0" (p. 8): χ(0) = 0 is automatic for an order-preserving bijection of [0, ∞); harmless. "The frequency has three letters" (p. 8) is an honest confession, not an error.

Uses of § 2 checked elsewhere: Theorem 3.5's proof (p. 64) uses C1 with b_n = 0 and a null array built from (K2) by Lévy continuity and the portmanteau inequality, as § 2.4 announces; Lemma C.1(2) (p. 72) uses C4 in the weak form only, as § 2.4 says; Proposition 4.11(1) is the only entry of C3 (p. 13, p. 104). Consistent.

## 5. The trust base (§ 1.1, pp. 6–7, read with Appendix I, pp. 103–105)

The account is unusually candid (the four cited facts, the two departures from their letter, the AI review, the toolchain pins and the axiom-guard output). Four points need attention.

1. **What is claimed as checked is stated in two different scopes.** § 1.1: "Every definition, lemma, proposition and theorem of §§ 3–4 and of the appendices". Appendix I: "The theorems are machine-checked ... So is every proposition and lemma their proofs spend, and every further result of the appendices." The second reading would exclude, for example, Propositions 4.17, 4.18 and 4.22 and Lemma 4.1, which no theorem's proof spends. The list of twenty sketched statements includes 4.18, so the first reading is presumably intended. Align the wording (say "every numbered statement of §§ 3–4 and Appendices A–H").

2. **The status of the line paper's statements is unclear.** § 1.1 says the statements restated in § 2.2 and "the two restated inside the proof of Proposition A.3" ([15, Prop. 6.6] and the fact of [15, Lem. 6.3], p. 62) "are proved in its released version ... and [the development requires] no formal development of the line paper". Appendix I says Proposition A.3 is machine-checked "on Lean's logical axioms alone". Those two sentences are consistent only if the formal proof of A.3 reproves what it takes from [15], or does not need it; the text does not say which. Likewise Theorem 2.5 is said to be "machine-checked in the same development" resting on C2 at d = 1, so the shared development must contain a line classification in some form. Required: one sentence stating, for each statement of § 2.2 and for the two restated in A.3's proof, whether it is reproved in the shared development, assumed, or not used by any formal proof.

3. **Sketched proofs of headline statements.** § 1.1 and Appendix I say the complete proofs of twenty statements are "in the LaTeX sources deposited with this version". A journal reader has the printed proofs, and the machine check is not a substitute for them (the formal and printed proofs are different objects, and the comparison of statements was done by AI agents, as the paper says). Among the sketched statements are Proposition 4.24 (p. 53), which carries the abstract's "four times the eccentricity" claim, Proposition A.3 (p. 62), which carries the "isotropic corner" claim of § 1, and Proposition 4.4 (p. 36). Required for a journal version: print complete proofs of the sketched statements that headline claims cite, in the paper or a supplement that is part of the submission.

4. **Minor.** The abstract's "The results are machine-checked" should read "The theorems of §§ 3–4" or similar, since § 2.2, the remarks (3.13, 3.14, 4.9, 4.16), and the numerics are not. § 1.1's "Every definition, lemma, proposition and theorem" should say "numbered statement" and note that remarks are not covered. The axiom name `gelfand_naimark_logMajorized` (p. 105) suggests a log-majorization (with the equality of totals), whereas § 2.4 says C4 states only the inequalities; confirm that the Lean axiom states the weak form that the paper calls C4.

Nothing is claimed as verified that the text elsewhere qualifies, with the one caveat in point 2.

## 6. Novelty and relation to prior work (as positioned in § 1)

- **Iijima via Weickert, Ishikawa and Imiya (p. 5, and Remark 3.14, p. 26).** The manuscript says Iijima's scale "is a positive definite 2 × 2 matrix", the transformations are "closed under every invertible linear map, and any two of them compose to a third", citing [59] p. 241. I could not reach the article; its published abstract describes a 1962 derivation of the *one-dimensional* Gaussian scale space from five axioms (linearity, translation invariance, scale invariance, semigroup property, positivity) and attributes a two-dimensional axiomatic to Otsu (1981). The matrix-scale reading may well be in the paper's account of Iijima's 1963 work, but since § 1 and Remark 3.14 build a comparison on it ("closer in form", "incomparable"), the author should quote the passage. Unverified, not wrong.
- **Attribution of the affine Gaussian scale space (p. 1).** "The affine Gaussian scale space of Lindeberg and Gårding makes the shape a parameter." Lindeberg's 1994 book (ch. 15, cited on p. 2) and Nitzberg and Shiota (1992), whom Weickert's § 1.2.6 (cited on p. 2) names, precede or parallel the ECCV 1994 paper. Suggest citing Nitzberg and Shiota alongside.
- **"Neither the order of a linear family nor its group is posed as a choice" (p. 5).** The claim is scoped to "the scale-space and vision work read for this paper", and the probability and Lie-theory neighbours where an order is a choice (Pedersen–Sato's cone parameter, Lawson's ordered homogeneous spaces) are acknowledged on pp. 6 and 36. Within vision the claim is defensible. One check: the paper says the order "appears in his later work as the meaning of a larger matrix scale ([35], fn. 7, p. 594)" (p. 26); Lindeberg's 2011 axiomatics [34] already treats the affine scale space as a semigroup over covariance matrices (§ 4.1 there, if I recall it correctly), in which case [34] should be cited for the order too.
- **Kelker's lemma (p. 6).** Secondary sources attribute "a spherical vector with independent components is normal" to Kelker (1970); the numbering "Lemma 5, p. 423" is unverified but consistent with the venue (Sankhyā A 32(4):419–430).
- **Pedersen and Sato (p. 6).** The comparison (additivity over the cone, moment and covariance hypotheses, versus covariance under GL(d) and the cascade) is a fair statement of a different rigidity; the page citations are plausible for JMSJ 56(2):541–559 and TJM 26(2):503–525 but unverified.
- **Matérn (p. 3, p. 6).** Correct; the law (1 + |ω|²)^{-γ} is the Matérn covariance only for γ > d/2 (said on p. 37, not on p. 3). The remark on p. 6 that no change of the shape matrix other than a rescaling is a further smoothing is Proposition 4.17 exactly.
- **Hemigroup (p. 3).** The term "convolution hemigroup" is Hazod–Siebert's; the definition number is unverified.
- Nothing in §§ 1–2 is claimed as new that I know to be known; the novelty claims about orders and cones (p. 36) belong to the § 4 block.

## 7. Exposition and structure (§§ 1–2)

- **§ 1 is too long for a journal** (six pages before § 1.1). The paragraphs "Relation to scale-space theory" (p. 5) and "Relation to probability and to matrix analysis" (p. 6) repeat § 3.5 (pp. 24–26), Remark 3.13–3.14, and the "Prior art" paragraphs of §§ 4.4 and 4.6 almost item by item (Iijima, Lindeberg's Theorem 5, AGLM, the α-scale spaces, Kelker, Pedersen–Sato, Urbanik, Jurek–Mason). Keep one copy, in § 3.5 and § 4, and reduce § 1 to the three questions and the three readings of the table (about three pages).
- **Definitions used before they are given**, all in § 1: "self-decomposable" (p. 2, "in the sense defined below"; defined p. 9), "affine orbit" (p. 3, defined informally there and formally p. 36), "stable orders ≤_α" (p. 3; § 4.3), "exponent" and "exponent spread" (p. 5; § 2.2 and Proposition 4.24). Acceptable for an introduction if each carries its forward reference; "exponent" on p. 5 does not.
- **Load-bearing forward references** in § 1: Propositions 4.5 and 4.4(3) (p. 2), Proposition 4.7 (p. 3), Proposition 4.22 and Lemma 3.9 (p. 4), Proposition F.3(4) (p. 5). These are fine, but the last points into an appendix for a statement that Table 2 should carry (see § 2 above).
- **Where the paper leans on the earlier papers.** § 3.1 (p. 19) and Remark 3.15 (p. 26) use the "variance gauge φ = χ²" of [15, Rem. 6.7] and the coordinate of [15, Prop. 6.6] before either is explained (the explanation is on p. 60). Put a one-line definition of the variance gauge in § 2.2 after Theorem 2.3. Otherwise § 2 does make the statements of the paper readable without [15]–[17]; the proofs rest on theirs, as § 2 says.
- **§ 2.3's subordination paragraph** (pp. 10–11, the delay datum and (2.6)) serves only the examples "every subordinated isotropic datum whose delay profile is bounded at the origin" (p. 46). It could be reduced to one sentence with (2.6) moved to Appendix E.
- **Table 1 and the list of letters with second meanings** (pp. 13–15) are useful; the list would be more useful as notes under the table than as prose spread across a page break. The three frequency letters (ω, ξ, z) are documented but a journal version could use ω throughout § 2.3 and say once that [16] writes ξ.
- The sentence on p. 6, "Through the singular matrix I − e₁e₁ᵀ the cascade makes the first coordinate of μ_I independent of the others", cannot be followed until Remark 3.13 (p. 25); either say "(Remark 3.13 explains)" or drop it from § 1.
- **What a journal version should lose from this block:** the two "Relation to" paragraphs of § 1 (to § 3.5/§ 4), the subordination paragraph of § 2.3 (to Appendix E), and the list of letters (into Table 1). § 2 itself is the right length for a paper meant to be read on its own.
