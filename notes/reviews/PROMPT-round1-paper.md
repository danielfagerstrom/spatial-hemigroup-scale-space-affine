## Part A, a content review

You are a referee for a mathematical-imaging journal (JMIV class), reviewing the attached
manuscript, *Spatial scale space from hemigroup axioms: affine covariance and shape adaptation*.
It belongs to a series by the same author. Earlier papers, cited as the line paper, the cone paper
and the isotropic paper, classify the admissible smoothing kernels on the real line and the
rotation-invariant ones on $\mathbb{R}^d$; this one indexes the kernels by covariance matrices and
asks which families an affine scale space can have. Section 2 restates what it uses of the earlier
papers, and the manuscript is meant to be readable on its own: say where it is not. Write a
detailed referee report. Be specific, cite pages of the PDF, and separate what is wrong from what
is merely improvable.

1. **Overall assessment and recommendation** (accept, minor revision, major revision, reject), with
   the reasons in a paragraph.

2. **The headline claims against what is proved.** The abstract, the paragraphs of section 1 from
   "What is an affine scale space?" to "Smaller groups", Table 2 (the landscape), Table 3 (the
   speed limit) and section 5 make claims; check each against the statements and proofs that are
   supposed to carry it. Say where a claim is stronger than its result, where a qualification made
   in the body is missing from the headline, and where a scope statement leaves a wrong impression.
   The claims most at risk:
   - that the main result is a classification of the families of three groups along three kinds of
     order, "completely in some cases": which cells of Table 2 are complete descriptions, and does
     the text say so cell by cell;
   - that for the general linear group one rank-deficient step from a positive definite matrix
     admits the Gaussian alone (Theorems 3.6 and 3.7), on kernels and on operators (Theorem 3.12);
   - that shape-adapted apertures are such steps, and that among linear images of rotation-invariant
     laws with a positive transform only the Gaussian reaches them by further smoothing
     (Proposition 4.22);
   - the speed limit: that along the chain of orders generated by the cones $C_\alpha$ the linear
     images of the isotropic $\gamma$-stable law cascade along the order of $C_\alpha$ exactly when
     $\gamma \ge \alpha$, and only the Gaussian from $\alpha = 2$ on (Theorem 4.21, Table 3);
   - that the kernels one can compute cascade at most along rays: the Matérn laws
     (Propositions 4.15 and 4.17), the filters whose symbol is a decaying rational function without
     zeros (Proposition 4.18), and the profiles bounded at the origin (Proposition 4.19);
   - that with the similarities non-Gaussian families cascade along the Loewner order
     (Proposition 4.20, Appendix H), and every sentence that reads the smaller groups as showing
     that "the rigidity is a property of the group";
   - every sentence that reads a statement for imaging (shape adaptation, re-measurement, a "price"
     in turning speed), and the scope sentences of section 1 and section 5.

3. **Mathematical correctness.** Read the statements and their proofs; many proofs are sketched in
   the body and complete in the appendices. Report every gap, error or unjustified step, with the
   page. Section 1.1 says that the results are machine-checked in Lean 4 on four cited theorems
   (C1 to C4, section 2.4). The printed proofs were written separately from the formal ones and are
   what a reader has, so read them as if nothing were machine-checked, and say where a printed proof
   does not prove the printed statement. Pay particular attention to:
   - Theorem 3.7 (the single rank-deficient step) and Theorem 3.6: the reduction of an affine
     cascade family to one function of one variable, the lemma on a characteristic function equal
     to 1 on a subspace, and the use of Kelker's lemma and Cauchy's equation;
   - Theorem 3.5 (a continuous cascade has infinitely divisible increments), its path condition,
     and its use of Khintchine's theorem (C1);
   - Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 4.3 (invariant orders and their cones): the general linear group
     is not connected, the orders are taken without a closure, and the cones need not be pointed;
     check what is claimed against the Lie-semigroup sources the text names;
   - Proposition 4.7 (the stable step cone) with Lemma 4.8 (the tail of an increment), and
     Proposition 4.13 (the step cone of an orbit), including what is left open for a general
     profile;
   - Propositions 4.10 and 4.11 (the exponent cone and the anisotropy budget);
   - Theorem 4.21 (the wedge axis), whose complete proof is Appendix C;
   - Proposition 4.15 (orbits of logarithmic exponent cascade along rays only): the
     dilation-invariant positive functional;
   - Proposition 4.20 and Appendix H (the similarities with the Loewner order);
   - the four cited facts C1 to C4, each checked against its stated hypotheses where it is used.

4. **The numerical examples.** Remark E.5 measures on a grid the negative mass of a Matérn step
   across shapes, and calls its growth in $\log t$ a numerical finding, not proved; Remark H.3
   computes a would-be increment by the fast Fourier transform; Figures 1 to 3 are drawn by
   scripts. Are the checks well designed, do the reported numbers support what the text
   concludes, and is anything concluded that a computation cannot show? If you can, reproduce the
   slopes of Remark E.5 and the sign test of Remark H.3. Is each computation described well enough
   to be reproduced?

5. **The trust base.** Section 1.1 and Appendix I state what the machine-checked development
   assumes and what is not formalized. Is the account clear, complete and honest as a reader would
   judge it? Is anything claimed as verified that the text elsewhere qualifies?

6. **Novelty and relation to prior work.** Check the positioning against the prior work the paper
   names: Iijima's matrix-scale axiomatic as Weickert, Ishikawa and Imiya report it; Lindeberg
   (1994, 2011, 2013) and Lindeberg and Gårding on shape adaptation; the morphological affine
   scale space of Alvarez, Guichard, Lions and Morel; the $\alpha$-scale spaces; Kelker's lemma;
   Urbanik, Jurek and Mason on operator-self-decomposable laws; Lawson and Hilgert, Hofmann and
   Lawson on ordered homogeneous spaces; Pedersen and Sato on cone-parameter semigroups; the
   anisotropic Matérn covariance of spatial statistics (Whittle; Lindgren, Rue and Lindström). Is
   anything claimed as new that is known, attributed wrongly, or missing? In particular, is it
   right that no prior work treats the order and the group of a linear family as a choice?

7. **Exposition and structure.** Where does the argument become hard to follow, where is a
   definition used before it is given, where is a forward reference load-bearing, where does the
   paper lean on an earlier paper, and what should a journal version lose? The paper is long
   (about 55 pages of text and 48 of appendices): say what a journal version should move, shorten
   or drop.

8. **A numbered list of required changes** and a separate **numbered list of suggestions**, each
   item with a page reference and one sentence of reason.

Do not summarize the paper back to the author beyond what the assessment needs. Do not soften
findings; a wrong claim should be called wrong. If you are unsure whether something is an error,
say so and say what would settle it.
