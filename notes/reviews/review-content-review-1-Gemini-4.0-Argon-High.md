### 1. Overall assessment and recommendation

**Recommendation:** **Minor-to-Major Revision** (mathematically sound and suitable for publication in *JMIV* after substantive structural compression and correction of several overclaimed headline statements).

**Reasons:** This manuscript makes a genuinely original and mathematically rigorous contribution to scale-space theory and applied probability. By framing linear scale spaces on $\mathbb{R}^d$ over covariance-matrix apertures $\Sigma \in \mathrm{PSD}(d)$ in terms of a covariance group $G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(d)$ and a cascade partial order $W$ ("wedge datum"), the author resolves a natural question at the intersection of Lindeberg and Gårding's affine scale space and the $\alpha$-scale spaces of Pauwels et al. and Duits et al. The main results—that a single rank-deficient Loewner step from a positive definite matrix forces the Gaussian among $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariant orbits (Theorems 3.6 and 3.7), that Lindeberg and Gårding's least-eigenvalue normalization for shape adaptation is precisely such a rank-deficient step (Proposition 4.22), that along the chain of $O(d)$-invariant cones $C_\alpha$ the isotropic $\gamma$-stable orbit cascades along $\preceq_{C_\alpha}^+$ if and only if $\gamma \ge \alpha$ while non-Gaussian families vanish at $\alpha = 2$ due to an operator-self-decomposability anisotropy budget (Proposition 4.11 and Theorem 4.21), and that Matérn and decaying zero-free rational symbols in $-\nabla\cdot\Sigma\nabla$ cascade only along rays (Propositions 4.15–4.18)—are both surprising and mathematically deep. The proofs in the manuscript are correct, and the Lean 4 formalization (resting on four clearly isolated literature theorems C1–C4) sets a very high standard of reliability. However, a revision is required for three reasons: (1) several headline claims in the Abstract, §1, Table 2, and §5 overstate what is proved or omit essential hypotheses (notably claiming a "classification" when 7 of the 9 cells of Table 2 are incomplete, stating Proposition 4.22(3) in the Abstract as if any order with a rank-deficient step admits the Gaussian even when it is not contained in the Loewner order, and stating the rational-symbol result in the Abstract without restricting to $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariant affine orbits); (2) several proofs in the printed text are only sketches whose complete versions are relegated to external files, and one main-text proposition (Proposition 4.20) has a load-bearing forward reference to Appendix H in its formal statement; and (3) at 108 pages, the manuscript suffers from heavy internal repetition (explaining each result four to five times and printing both long sketches and full proofs of the same theorems) and needs substantial condensation for a journal article.

### 2. The headline claims against what is proved

1. **The claim of a "classification" across the three groups and three orders (Abstract, p. 1; §1, p. 3; Table 2, p. 31; §5, p. 55):**
   - **What is claimed:** The Abstract (p. 1) states that *"The main result classifies the families of three groups (general linear, similarities, positive diagonal) along the ray, stable and Loewner orders, completely in some cases."* Section 5 (p. 55) claims: *"Within these limits two descriptions stop short of a classification: the orbit of a general profile is described by a sufficient condition, and the similarity row through bounds on the shape of its kernels."*
   - **Against what is proved:** Out of the nine cells in the $3 \times 3$ grid of Table 2 (p. 31), **only two cells**—$(\mathrm{GL}(d), \text{rays})$ (Propositions 3.4(3) and 4.5) and $(\mathrm{GL}(d), \text{Loewner})$ (Theorem 3.6)—are complete classifications on $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$. A third cell, $(D_+(d), \text{Loewner})$, is classified only on the positive definite diagonal matrices ("the frame's shapes", Propositions F.2 and F.4(3)), not on all of $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$. The remaining six cells are incomplete.
   - **Where the text misleads or omits qualifications:**
     - **Section 5 (p. 55) is wrong by omission:** The sentence *"Within these limits two descriptions stop short of a classification..."* omits that (i) $(S(d), \text{rays})$ stops short of classifying which choices of self-decomposable laws on the simplex $\Delta_d$ extend continuously to $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$ (Open Problem 4, p. 55); (ii) $(D_+(d), \text{rays})$ and $(D_+(d), \text{Loewner})$ do not classify the families off the diagonal matrices over $\mathrm{Corr}(d)$ (Open Problem 4, p. 55); and (iii) $(D_+(d), \le_\alpha)$ does not even classify the families *on* the frame's shapes, because Proposition F.5(2) (p. 91) proves only necessity ($\mu_I$ is $B$-self-decomposable for all $B \in \mathcal{D}_\alpha$) while its converse is open (Open Problem 4, p. 55).
     - **Table 2, cell $(D_+(d), \le_\alpha)$ (p. 31) is misleading:** It reads *"on the frame's shapes $(D_s)_*\mu_I$ with $\mu_I$ operator-self-decomposable for every diagonal exponent in $K_\alpha$; ... off the frame's shapes not described (Proposition F.5)"*, which presents the necessary condition on the frame's shapes as if it were a characterization, without noting in the cell that the converse is open.
     - **Table 2, cell $(D_+(d), \text{Loewner})$ (p. 31):** States *"on the frame's shapes the products $(s_1)_*\nu_1 \otimes \dots \otimes (s_d)_*\nu_d$ of self-decomposable laws on the line"*, without noting in the cell that off the frame's shapes only one extension ($\Sigma \mapsto (\Delta(\Sigma)^{1/2})_*(\nu_1 \otimes \dots \otimes \nu_d)$) is given and the general classification off the diagonal is open.

2. **One rank-deficient step for $\mathrm{GL}(d)$ (Abstract, p. 1; §1, pp. 3–4; Theorems 3.6, 3.7, 3.12, pp. 21, 23; Proposition 4.22(3), p. 50; §5, p. 54):**
   - **What is claimed:** The Abstract (p. 1) states: *"For the general linear group, an order with one step from a positive definite matrix that widens the aperture in some directions and leaves it unchanged in the others admits the Gaussian alone."* Section 1 (pp. 3–4) states: *"so for the general linear group every order of the scheme that contains one rank-deficient step admits only Gaussian families (Proposition 4.22(3))."*
   - **Against what is proved:** Theorem 3.6 (p. 21) and Theorem 3.12 (p. 23) prove the exact characterization for $(\mathrm{GL}(d), \text{Loewner})$. Theorem 3.7 (p. 21) proves that for a single step $\Sigma \to \Sigma + P$ ($\Sigma \in \mathrm{PD}(d)$, $0 < \operatorname{rank} P < d$), $e^{-g(\langle\omega,(\Sigma+P)\omega\rangle)} = e^{-g(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)}\widehat{\rho}(\omega)$ forces $g(u) = bu$. Proposition 4.22(3) (p. 50) proves a *necessity* statement: if $(\mathrm{GL}(d), W)$ is a wedge datum with $wI \le_W wM$ ($\lambda_{\min}(M) = 1$, $M \ne I$), then any $(\mathrm{GL}(d), W)$-cascade family has $\widehat{\mu}_{\Sigma,\Sigma'}(\omega) = e^{-b\langle\omega, (\Sigma'-\Sigma)\omega\rangle}$.
   - **Where the headline claim is stronger than the result:** In the Abstract (p. 1) and §1 (p. 4), saying that any such order $W$ *"admits the Gaussian alone"* is false unless $W$ is **contained in the Loewner order**. If $W$ contains a rank-deficient Loewner step $wI \le_W wM$ *and* also contains a step $\Sigma \le_W \Sigma'$ where $\Sigma' - \Sigma$ is not positive semidefinite, then $e^{-b\langle\omega,(\Sigma'-\Sigma)\omega\rangle}$ is not a characteristic function and $W$ **admits no family at all** (as in Theorem 4.21(2)(a), p. 48). The Abstract and §1 must say *"admits at most the Gaussian (and admits the Gaussian when the order is contained in the Loewner order)"*. Moreover, §1 (p. 3) should note when citing Theorem 3.7 that the single-step theorem assumes the affine-orbit form $\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma(\omega) = e^{-g(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)}$, which in the scheme comes from $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariance together with the ray cascade.

3. **Shape-adapted apertures as rank-deficient steps (Abstract, p. 1; §1, p. 4; §4.1, p. 26; Proposition 4.22, p. 50; §5, p. 54):**
   - **Assessment:** This claim is completely supported by Proposition 4.22(1)–(2) (p. 50). The qualification in the Abstract (*"among linear images of rotation-invariant laws with a positive transform only the Gaussian reaches them by further smoothing"*) and in §1 (p. 4) matches the hypotheses of Proposition 4.22(2) and Proposition 4.4 exactly. The imaging caveat—that Lindeberg and Gårding's iterative algorithm re-convolves the original image at every stage and does not use a cascade between iterations (§1, p. 4; §4.1, p. 26; §5, p. 54)—is stated clearly and honestly.
   - **Terminology error in §4.8 (p. 51):** In the worked slant example for the $\alpha$-stable orbit ($0 < \alpha < 2$) on p. 51, the author writes *"w and s are variances, not volume scales. At the boundary the variance along the minor axis is $n_1^{-d/\alpha}$ times that of the first stage."* For $0 < \alpha < 2$, an $\alpha$-stable law has **infinite variance**; $w$ and $s$ are matrix scale parameters in the variance gauge, not variances.

4. **The speed limit and the cone chain $C_\alpha$ (Abstract, p. 1; §1, p. 4; Table 3, p. 52; Theorem 4.21, pp. 48–49):**
   - **Assessment:** The mathematical claims in the Abstract, §1, Table 3, and §5 regarding $C_\alpha$, the threshold $\gamma \ge \alpha$ for $0 < \alpha, \gamma < 2$, and Gaussian rigidity for $2 \le \alpha \le \infty$ match Theorem 4.21(3) (pp. 48–49) and Proposition 4.7(3) (p. 38) accurately.
   - **Improvable/confusing terminology ("turning speed"):** In the Abstract (p. 1) and §1 (pp. 3–4), the author uses *"turning speed"* and *"how fast the shape may turn"* for the bound $\sqrt{n_d/n_1} \le (t_2/t_1)^{d\alpha/(d+\alpha)}$ of Proposition 4.7(3). Geometrically, this bound restricts the relative shape eigenvalues $n_i$ of $S_1^{-1/2} S_2 S_1^{-1/2}$, which limits *both* coaxial stretching (eccentricity growth without rotating the axes, as when starting from the round aperture $S_1 = I$ in Figure 2, p. 29) *and* rotation of the principal axes of an already-anisotropic ellipse. Yet on p. 52 (Table 3) and p. 53 (§4.9), *"turning"* is used in its literal sense (rotating the frame's axes) to say that diagonal frame families *"gain anisotropy along its axes as fast per step as the Gaussian does and cannot turn"*. Using "turning" to mean *all* shape change in §1 and the Abstract, and then using it to mean *axis rotation as opposed to coaxial stretching* in §4.9 and Table 3, is inconsistent.

5. **Kernels that cascade at most along rays: Matérn, decaying rational symbols, bounded profiles (Abstract, p. 1; §1, p. 4; Propositions 4.15, 4.17, 4.18, 4.19, pp. 44–46; §5, p. 54):**
   - **Missing hypothesis in the Abstract (p. 1) and §5 (p. 54):** The Abstract states: *"Filters whose symbol in $-\nabla\cdot\Sigma\nabla$ is a decaying rational function without zeros cascade at most along rays, and so do the linear images of a Matérn law."* Section 5 (p. 54) repeats: *"The filters whose symbol in the shape's operator is a decaying rational function without zeros on $[0,\infty)$, which finitely many elliptic solves compute, keep none across shapes: they cascade at most along rays (Proposition 4.18)."*
   - **Against what is proved:** Proposition 4.18 (p. 45) proves this **only for affine orbits** $\mu_\Sigma = (\Sigma^{1/2})_*\mu_I$, where $\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma(\omega) = Q(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)/P(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)$ for **fixed** polynomials $P, Q$ independent of $\Sigma$ (as forced by $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariance). Notice that the Abstract explicitly says *"the linear images of a Matérn law"* for Matérn, but omits *"the linear images of"* (or *"among affine orbits"*) for rational symbols! Without $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariance (for example, if the rational function coefficients depend on $\Sigma$ under $S(d)$, or if elliptic solves $(I - s_i^2 \partial_i^2)^{-1}$ are applied axis by axis as in Proposition F.2), non-ray cascades exist. Both the Abstract (p. 1) and §5 (p. 54) must specify that this applies to **affine orbits** (or $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariant families) whose symbol is a fixed decaying zero-free rational function of $\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle$.

6. **Similarities with the Loewner order and "rigidity is a property of the group" (§1, p. 4; Table 2, p. 31; Proposition 4.20, pp. 46–47; §4.9, p. 53; Appendix H, pp. 94–102):**
   - **Assessment of the mathematical claim:** Proposition H.1(3) (p. 94) and Proposition 4.20(4) (p. 47) fully support the claim that non-Gaussian $(S(d), \text{Loewner})$-cascade families exist and satisfy $\operatorname{es}(\psi_\Sigma) \le 4\operatorname{ecc}(\Sigma)$.
   - **Two inaccuracies in the surrounding prose:**
     - On p. 4 (§1, "Smaller groups"), the author writes: *"So the rigidity of the general linear group with the Loewner order is a property of the group, and the Loewner order alone does not force the Gaussian."* This is a half-truth: $\mathrm{GL}(d)$ *alone* does not force the Gaussian either, since $(\mathrm{GL}(d), \le_\alpha)$ admits non-Gaussian families for all $0 < \alpha < 2$ (Theorem 4.21). Rigidity is a joint property of the *pair* $(\mathrm{GL}(d), \text{Loewner})$—specifically, $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariance combined with at least one rank-deficient step in the order.
     - In §4.9 (p. 53), the example from Proposition H.1(3) is stated with $b > 0$ ($\mu_\Sigma = N(0,2b\Sigma) * S_\alpha(\Sigma + \kappa\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)I)$), and Table 2 (p. 31) says *"not every family is a Gaussian convolved with a shape-blind part (Proposition H.1(3))"*. In fact, Proposition H.1(3) (p. 94) allows $b = 0$, so the purely non-Gaussian family $\mu_\Sigma = S_\alpha(\Sigma + \kappa\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)I)$ is already an $(S(d), \text{Loewner})$-cascade family with no Gaussian part at all. Writing $b > 0$ on p. 53 obscures this stronger fact.

### 3. Mathematical correctness

I have checked all statements and proofs in the main text (§§2–4) and Appendices A–H without relying on the Lean verification. The mathematical arguments are sound and free of fatal flaws. Below is the detailed audit of the specific components and the minor discrepancies found in the printed proofs:

1. **Theorem 3.7, Theorem 3.6, Theorem 3.12, and Lemmas 3.8–3.10 (pp. 21–23, 56–60, 65–66):**
   - **Correctness:** Complete and rigorous. Lemma 3.8 (p. 65) uses a clean connectedness and separability argument on $U \subset H$ to show $\widehat{\rho}|_U = 1 \implies \operatorname{supp}(\rho) \subseteq H^\perp$. Lemma 3.9 (pp. 65–66) uses $O(d)$-invariance at $I$, $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-transport on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$, density of $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ in $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$ with (K2), and a first-zero argument along the ray $tI$ to establish $\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma(\omega) = e^{-g(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)}$ with $g \ge 0$ continuous and $g(0)=0$. Lemma 3.10 (p. 66) deduces additivity of $g$ from $g(au+w)-g(u+w) = g(au)-g(u)$ ($a > 1$) using only continuity at $0$, and then uses $g \ge 0$ to get monotonicity and linearity $g(u) = bu$. Theorem 3.7 (p. 22) reduces the single rank-deficient step $\Sigma \to \Sigma + P$ ($0 < \operatorname{rank} P < d$) to Lemma 3.8 on $H = \ker(Q^T\Sigma^{-1/2}P\Sigma^{-1/2}Q)$ and Lemma 3.10. Lemmas A.1 and A.2 (pp. 56–60) rigorously establish the equivalence between operator families on $L^1(\mathbb{R}^d)$ and kernel families via Bochner integration, Prokhorov tightness of smoothed box approximants, and Gaussian multiplier smoothing, carrying Theorem 3.6 to Theorem 3.12.

2. **Theorem 3.5 (infinitely divisible increments, pp. 19–20, 64–65):**
   - **Correctness:** Complete and rigorous. Uniform continuity of $(a,b) \mapsto \widehat{\mu}_{a,b}(\omega)$ on $\{0 \le a \le b \le 1\}$, Lévy's continuity theorem, and the portmanteau theorem establish that equal subdivisions of $[s,t]$ form a null array, and C1 (Khintchine's theorem, Theorem 2.10) with $b_n = 0$ yields infinite divisibility. In Clause (3)(c) (p. 65), the verification that $\zeta(s) = \Sigma^{1/2} M^s \Sigma^{1/2}$ is a $\mathcal{K}$-chain uses the polar factor $Q = \zeta(s)^{-1/2}\Sigma^{1/2}M^{s/2} \in O(d)$ to show $\zeta(s)^{-1/2}\zeta(t)\zeta(s)^{-1/2} = Q M^{t-s} Q^T \in \mathcal{K}$. (Note: the last sentence of the proof of (3)(c) on p. 65 forward-references Proposition 4.7(2) for the transitivity of $\le_{\mathcal{K}_\alpha}$.)

3. **Proposition 4.2, Lemma C.1, Theorem 4.3, and Proposition 4.12 (invariant orders and cone fields, pp. 33–35, 41–42, 70–75):**
   - **Correctness:** This is one of the strongest technical parts of the paper. Because $\mathrm{GL}(d)$ has two connected components and step composition $(M,N) \mapsto M^{1/2} Q^T N Q M^{1/2}$ depends on an arbitrary relative eigenframe $Q \in O(d)$, standard Lie-semigroup theorems for connected groups or pointed cones do not apply off the shelf. In Lemma C.1 (pp. 70–72) and Theorem 4.3 (pp. 72–74), the author gives a completely self-contained proof on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ using Ky Fan's extremal principle for sums and the Gel'fand–Naimark–Lidskii log-majorization inequality (C4, Theorem 2.13) for products, showing that the dual functionals $F_w(\Sigma,\Sigma') = \sum_{i=1}^d w_i^\downarrow \ell_i(\Sigma^{-1/2}\Sigma'\Sigma^{-1/2})$ ($w \in D^\#$) are superadditive along any three points and nondecreasing along any $C$-causal curve. This proves $\preceq_C = \le_{\exp C}$ directly without needing a closure or connectedness of $\mathrm{GL}(d)$ or pointedness of $C$.

4. **Proposition 4.7, Lemma 4.8, and Proposition 4.13 (step cones of orbits, pp. 38–39, 42–43, 77–78):**
   - **Correctness:** The proofs of Lemma 4.8 (p. 77), Proposition 4.7 (p. 39), and Proposition 4.13 (pp. 77–78) are complete and correct. What is left open for a general nonincreasing radial profile $k$—whether a positive increment $\mu_{\Sigma'} = \mu_\Sigma * \rho$ forces pointwise ordering $n_\Sigma \le n_{\Sigma'}$ of the Lévy densities when $k$ is neither stable nor bounded at the origin—is stated accurately on pp. 43, 49, and 55 (Open Problem 1).

5. **Propositions 4.10 and 4.11 (exponent cone and anisotropy budget, pp. 40, 68, 79–80):**
   - **Correctness:** The proofs of Proposition 4.10 (p. 68) and Proposition 4.11(2) (p. 68) are complete. The proof of Proposition 4.11(1) (pp. 79–80)—showing that any rotation-invariant non-Gaussian $B$-self-decomposable law with $e^{-tB} \to 0$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr} B < (d+2)\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym} B)$—is very well executed: it uses C3 (Theorem 2.12) to get infinite divisibility and $(e^{-tB})_*\nu \le \nu$ for the Lévy measure $\nu$, smooths $\nu$ radially by a compactly supported bump $\eta$ so that no Haar measure on $O(d)$ or radial density assumption on $\nu$ is needed, differentiates along a least eigenvector of $\operatorname{sym} B$ to obtain the differential inequality $\operatorname{tr} B\, M(r) + \lambda r M'(r) \le 0$, and uses $\int_{|x|\le 1} |x|^2 \nu_\eta(dx) < \infty$ to force $\operatorname{tr} B/\lambda < d+2$.

6. **Theorem 4.21 (the general linear row along the chain of cones, pp. 48–50, 80–82):**
   - **Correctness:** The complete proof in Appendix C.4 (pp. 80–82) is thorough and correct.

7. **Proposition 4.15 (orbits of logarithmic exponent cascade along rays only, p. 44):**
   - **Gap in the printed text (no complete proof in the PDF):** On p. 44, Proposition 4.15 only has a *"Sketch"*, and unlike Lemma 4.8 or Proposition 4.13, **it has no complete proof in Appendix B or Appendix C**. While the sketch outlines a valid distributional scaling argument as $R \downarrow 0$, it:
     (i) abruptly switches Fourier transform conventions to $\Phi(\xi) = \int e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\phi(x)dx$ (everywhere else in the paper uses $e^{\pm i\langle\omega,x\rangle}$ without $2\pi$); and
     (ii) asserts in one phrase (*"Testing with $\chi_0(x/N)\cos(2\pi\langle a,x\rangle)$, $\chi_0$ a bump equal to $1$ near $0$, and letting $N \to \infty$ gives $h(a) = c_0$ for every $a \ne 0$"*) the approximate-identity step on the Fourier side without writing out why $\int \Phi_N(\xi)h(\xi)d\xi \to h(a)$.
     Because Proposition 4.15 is a headline theorem of the main text (on which Propositions 4.17, 4.18, 4.19, E.2, and H.2(4) all depend), its complete proof must be printed in the paper.

8. **Proposition 4.20 and Appendix H (the similarities with the Loewner order, pp. 46–47, 69, 94–102):**
   - **Typographical errors in Proposition H.5(1) and its proof sketch (pp. 97–98):**
     - In Proposition H.5(1) (p. 97, lines 2–3), the statement quantifies *"For $c > 0$ and $Q \in O(d)$"*, but then writes $\operatorname{rs}((cR)_*L) = \operatorname{rs}(L)$, $\operatorname{es}(\psi(cR\,\cdot)) = \operatorname{es}(\psi)$ and $\mathcal{M}_s((cR)_*L) = c^2 Q\,\mathcal{M}_{s/c}(L)\,Q^T$, with an undefined matrix $R$ in place of $Q$.

### 4. The numerical examples

1. **Design and validity of the conclusions:**
   - **Remark E.5 (p. 87):** Measures on a 2D periodic lattice the negative mass of the discrete Matérn increment from $t^2 I$ to $t^2\operatorname{diag}(1+\varepsilon, 1)$ for $t \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$, $\gamma \in \{1, 2\}$, and $\varepsilon \in \{0.01, 0.1, 0.5\}$. The analytical prediction from the continuum asymptotic of Proposition E.4(3)–(4) (p. 86–87), if the radial integral $\int_a^1 dr/r = \log(1/a)$ is cut off at the grid spacing $a \approx 1/t$, gives a slope in $\log t$ of $\mathcal{B}_2\Theta = \frac{\varepsilon}{\pi(1+\varepsilon)}$. Checking the arithmetic:
     - At $\varepsilon = 0.01$: $\frac{0.01}{1.01\pi} = 0.003152$ (reported continuum heuristic: $0.0032$; fitted lattice slope: $0.0030$).
     - At $\varepsilon = 0.1$: $\frac{0.1}{1.1\pi} = 0.028937$ (reported continuum heuristic: $0.0289$; fitted lattice slope: $0.0283$).
     - At $\varepsilon = 0.5$: $\frac{0.5}{1.5\pi} = 0.106103$ (reported continuum heuristic: $0.1061$; fitted lattice slope: $0.1064$).
     - For $\gamma = 2$, $2\mathcal{B}_2\Theta$ gives $0.0063, 0.0579, 0.2122$ against the fitted lattice slopes $0.0060, 0.0544, 0.1768$, confirming agreement to first order in $\varepsilon$ and departure at $\varepsilon = 0.5$.
     The author explicitly states that the $\log t$ growth on the lattice is a numerical finding and is not proved (p. 87, and Open Problem 6, p. 56).
   - **Remark H.3 (p. 96):** Tests the single step from $\Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(1, 0.3)$ to $\Sigma' = \operatorname{diag}(1.05, 0.85)$ ($P = \operatorname{diag}(0.05, 0.55)$) for the law $\mu_\Sigma = N(0, 2b\Sigma) * \nu_{h(\Sigma)}$, where $\nu_H$ is the index-1 Matérn law and $h(\Sigma) = \Sigma + 2\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)I$, so $h(\Sigma) = \operatorname{diag}(3.6, 2.9)$ and $h(\Sigma') = \operatorname{diag}(4.85, 4.65)$. The continuum Fourier transform of the would-be increment is
     $$\widehat{\rho}(\omega) = e^{-b(0.05\omega_1^2 + 0.55\omega_2^2)} \frac{1 + 3.6\omega_1^2 + 2.9\omega_2^2}{1 + 4.85\omega_1^2 + 4.65\omega_2^2}.$$
     Because $P \in \mathrm{PD}(2)$, the Gaussian factor $N(0, 2bP)$ smooths the logarithmic singularity of the Matérn quotient at the origin, while at large $|x|$ the exponentially decaying Matérn tail of $h(\Sigma')$ dominates the Gaussian tail and is positive. Thus for small $b = 0.1$ the quadrupole negative lobe near the origin along the $x_1$-axis survives (giving negative mass $\approx 10^{-2}$), whereas for $b = 2$ the Gaussian blur is wide enough to make the convolved density positive everywhere to double-precision rounding ($\min / \max \approx -10^{-17}$). The author is scrupulously careful to state (p. 20, p. 56, p. 96) that positivity on $\mathbb{R}^2$ at $b = 2$ is not proved, and correctly deduces from C2(ii) that *if* $\rho$ is positive, it cannot be infinitely divisible because its Lévy density $n_{h(\Sigma')} - n_{h(\Sigma)}$ takes negative values. Nothing is concluded that a computation cannot show.

2. **Reproducibility from the text:**
   - **Neither Remark E.5 nor Remark H.3 states the discretization parameters needed to reproduce the exact numbers without inspecting the external scripts:**
     - In **Remark E.5 (p. 87)**, the periodic grid size $N \times N$ of $\mathbb{Z}_N^2$ is not stated, and the exact stencil weights of *"the nine-point discretization of $\nabla\cdot S\nabla$"* are not given (for diagonal $S = \operatorname{diag}(s_1, s_2)$, standard central differences give a 5-point stencil unless a specific isotropic-anisotropic 9-point weighting is chosen).
     - In **Remark H.3 (p. 96)**, the spatial/frequency box size and the two grid resolutions at which the FFT was evaluated are not stated.
     Adding one sentence to each remark specifying the grid dimensions, frequency cutoff, and stencil weights will make both numerical checks fully reproducible from the printed page.

### 5. The trust base

The account of the formal verification in §1.1 (pp. 6–7), §2.4 (pp. 11–13), and Appendix I (pp. 102–104) is **clear, complete, and exemplary in its honesty**:
1. The trust boundary is stated explicitly: Lean 4's three standard logical axioms plus four named axioms (`khintchine_null_array`, `levy_khintchine_exists/unique/converse`, `operatorSelfDecomposable_infinitelyDivisible`, and `gelfand_naimark_logMajorized`) corresponding to C1–C4.

### 6. Novelty and relation to prior work

1. **Positioning against prior work:**
   - **Iijima (1962, 1963) via Weickert, Ishikawa, and Imiya [57]:** Accurately described in §1 (p. 5) and Remark 3.14 (p. 25). Iijima assumed an unordered composition law on positive definite $2\times 2$ matrices where any two members of the family convolve to a third member of the family; Theorem 3.6 requires only a hemigroup cascade along Loewner-ordered pairs and does not assume increments belong to the family $\{\mu_{0,\Sigma}\}$.
   - **Lindeberg (1994, 2011, 2013) [31, 32, 33] and Lindeberg & Gårding (1994, 1997) [34, 35]:** Accurately positioned. Connecting the minimum-eigenvalue normalization of shape adaptation ([34, p. 396; 35, §6.2]) to rank-deficient Loewner steps (Proposition 4.22) is novel and illuminating.
   - **Alvarez, Guichard, Lions, and Morel [1], Sapiro & Tannenbaum [50], and Olver, Sapiro, and Tannenbaum [42]:** Accurately contrasted in §1 (pp. 2, 5) and §3.5 (p. 24).
   - **$\alpha$-scale spaces (Pauwels et al. [43]; Duits et al. [13, 14]):** Accurately positioned in §1 (p. 5) and §3.5 (p. 24). You might also mention Felsberg and Sommer's work on anisotropic Poisson scale spaces (e.g., Felsberg's 2002 Kiel dissertation, where extending the Poisson kernel anisotropically along one direction while keeping the orthogonal scale fixed is discussed heuristically); Theorem 3.7 and Proposition 4.7(3) rigorously explain why such a rank-1 step has no positive convolution increment for the elliptical Poisson orbit.
   - **Kelker's lemma [28] and characterizations of the normal law among elliptically contoured distributions (Cambanis, Huang, and Simons [7]; Fang, Kotz, and Ng [18]; Khatri and Mukerjee [29]; Anderson and Fang [2]):** Remark 3.13 (p. 24) gives a thorough and fair account of why Kelker's lemma is the core of Theorem 3.6 and what the single-step formulation (Theorem 3.7) adds.
   - **Operator-self-decomposable laws (Urbanik [55]; Jurek [26]; Jurek and Mason [27]):** The "Prior art" paragraph on p. 41 accurately separates what is in Urbanik and Jurek–Mason (the Lie wedge of exponents, the Gaussian decomposability semigroup, and the Lévy measure condition $\nu(U) \ge \nu(e^{tB}U)$) from what is new here (the explicit exponent cone $K_\alpha$ and step cone $\preceq_\alpha$ of the elliptical stable orbit in Propositions 4.7 and 4.10, and the quantitative trace-to-minimum-eigenvalue budget $\operatorname{tr} B < (d+2)\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym} B)$ in Proposition 4.11).
   - **Ordered homogeneous spaces and Lie semigroups (Lawson [30]; Hilgert, Hofmann, and Lawson [21]; Hilgert and Ólafsson [22]):** The "Prior art" paragraph on p. 35 accurately credits the general Lie-semigroup framework and explains what is new in the majorization proof on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ (Theorem 4.3).
   - **Cone-parameter convolution semigroups (Pedersen and Sato [44, 45]):** Accurately contrasted on p. 5: Pedersen and Sato assume additivity $\mu_\Sigma * \mu_{\Sigma'} = \mu_{\Sigma+\Sigma'}$ over the cone $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$ and derive Gaussianity from finite second moments and a covariance bound, whereas Theorem 3.6 assumes neither additivity nor finite moments, deriving additivity and Gaussianity from $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariance and the Loewner cascade.
   - **Anisotropic Matérn covariances (Matérn [39]; Whittle [58]; Lindgren, Rue, and Lindström [36]):** Accurately cited on pp. 2, 6, 36, and 45.

2. **Is it right that no prior work treats the order and the group of a linear family as a choice?**
   - Yes, with the phrasing the author uses on p. 5 (*"In the scale-space and vision work read for this paper, neither the order of a linear family nor its group is posed as a choice"*). While covariance groups were systematically varied for *nonlinear* geometric PDEs by Olver, Sapiro, and Tannenbaum [42] (as the author notes on p. 5), and cone-parameter semigroups over general cones were studied in probability by Pedersen and Sato [44, 45] without a group action, varying the covariance subgroup $G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(d)$ and the $G$-invariant partial order $W$ on $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$ simultaneously to map out the admissible linear scale spaces is new.

### 7. Exposition and structure

1. **Symbols and concepts used before they are defined, and load-bearing forward references:**
   - **Abstract (p. 1):** Mentions *"generated by cones $C_\alpha$"* and *"For $0 < \alpha, \gamma < 2$... from $\alpha = 2$ on"* without defining $C_\alpha$ or explaining what the parameter $\alpha$ represents. A reader of the Abstract has no way to know what $C_\alpha$ is.
   - **Proposition 4.20(3)–(4) (pp. 46–47):** This formal statement in §4.6 of the main text has a **load-bearing forward reference to Appendix H** (pp. 96–97): its preamble says *"use the functionals $\operatorname{es}, \mathcal{M}_s, T_s$ and $\operatorname{cond}$ of Proposition H.5"*, Clause (3) states that $(\mu_\Sigma)_{\Sigma\in\mathrm{PD}(d)}$ *"is a family as in Proposition H.4"*, and Clause (4) says *"with $\psi_\Sigma$ as in Proposition H.4"*. A formal proposition in the main text should be self-contained and not depend on definitions and standing hypotheses stated 50 pages later in an appendix.

   - Mathematically, §2 (pp. 7–11) and the two restated lemmas inside the proof of Proposition A.3 (p. 62) make the manuscript self-contained with respect to `[15]`, `[16]`, and `[17]`.

   - At **108 pages** (55 pages of main text and 48 pages of appendices), the manuscript is far too long for a standard journal paper in *JMIV*, and much of its length comes from structural repetition rather than mathematical necessity:

### 8. Required changes and suggestions

#### Required changes

1. **Abstract (p. 1) and §1 (p. 4):** Qualify the claim *"For the general linear group, an order with one step from a positive definite matrix that widens the aperture in some directions and leaves it unchanged in the others admits the Gaussian alone"* to state that such an order **contained in the Loewner order** admits the Gaussian alone (and admits *no* family if it is not contained in the Loewner order, since Proposition 4.22(3), p. 50, proves only necessity).
2. **Abstract (p. 1) and §5 (p. 54):** In the sentence *"Filters whose symbol in $-\nabla\cdot\Sigma\nabla$ is a decaying rational function without zeros cascade at most along rays"*, specify that this holds **among affine orbits** (or $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-covariant families, where the rational function $Q/P$ of $\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle$ has fixed coefficients independent of $\Sigma$), matching the hypothesis of Proposition 4.18 (p. 45).
3. **Abstract (p. 1):** Define the cones $C_\alpha$ (or state what the index $\alpha$ means, e.g., $C_\alpha = \{X \in \mathrm{PSD}(d) : (d+\alpha)\lambda_{\min}(X) \ge \operatorname{tr} X\}$) when $C_\alpha$ is introduced in the Abstract, so the Abstract is intelligible on its own.
