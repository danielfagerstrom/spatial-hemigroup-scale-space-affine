*Spatial scale space from hemigroup axioms: affine covariance and shape adaptation* (working draft, 111 pp.)

**Block reviewed.** Section 3 (pp. 15–26: the $(G,W)$ scheme, the parameter space, infinitely divisible increments, the single step and the rigidity, prior art) and Appendix A (pp. 56–66: operators and kernels, the kernels from scale zero, the isotropic corner, the parameter space, the proofs of Theorem 3.5 and of the three single-step lemmas), together with the proof of Proposition 3.4 in Appendix C.2 (pp. 76–77), which is a statement of § 3. The report follows the eight parts of the brief. Every finding is marked **wrong** (a statement that is false or a proof that does not prove its statement), **misleading** (true but likely to be read as more or less than it is) or **improvable**.

## 1. Overall assessment and recommendation

**Recommendation for this block: minor revision.**

The mathematics of § 3 and Appendix A is correct. I read every statement and every proof of the block as if nothing were machine-checked, and I found no gap, no false statement and no proof that proves less than its statement. The single-step theorem (Theorem 3.7, p. 21) is proved completely in print (pp. 23, 65–66): the transport to the identity, Lemma 3.8 (a transform equal to 1 on a subspace), the functional equation (3.5) and its solution in Lemma 3.10 from the sign of $g$ and continuity at $0$ alone are all right, and the rigidity on kernels (Theorem 3.6) and on operators (Theorem 3.12) follow as stated. Theorem 3.5 (pp. 20, 64–65) is Sato's argument for additive processes transplanted to a cascade with a path condition, and its proof is complete and correctly uses C1. Lemmas A.1 and A.2 (pp. 56–60) correctly carry the axioms between operators and kernels; Proposition A.3 (pp. 61–62) and Lemmas A.4–A.5 (pp. 62–64) are right.

What needs revision is of three kinds. (i) One sentence of the introduction about the single step is misleading (p. 4, "The base must be positive definite"), and the comparison with Iijima's axioms (Remark 3.14, pp. 25–26) understates the result: the continuity axiom (K2) enters Theorem 3.6 only at the boundary of $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$, and the Gaussian form of every kernel and increment between positive definite apertures follows from (K1), (K3) and (K4) alone; I give a family showing that (K2) is needed at the boundary and nowhere else. (ii) Two classical results are reproved without attribution: the representation of a bounded translation-commuting operator on $L^1$ as a convolution (Lemma A.1(1); Wendel 1952) and the compactness of the symmetry group of a full law (Lemma A.5(1); Billingsley 1966, Jurek and Mason). (iii) The verification account lists Proposition A.3 among the statements "machine-checked on Lean's logical axioms alone" although its printed proof rests on a restated statement of the line paper, which has no formal development; the reader cannot tell from Appendix I where that statement enters the formal proof. Besides these, the nondegeneracy axiom (K4) at the single pair $(0,I)$ admits, for the similarities, families whose kernel at a positive definite aperture is $\delta_0$; this is not an error, since every statement is relative to Definition 3.1, but a reader who takes "kernel" as "aperture that measures the image" should be told, and the choice should be defended or changed. The exposition of § 3 repeats one derivation four times and could lose a page and a half; § 3.5 could lose a page.

## 2. The headline claims against what is proved (as far as this block carries them)

The claims of the abstract, of § 1 up to "Smaller groups", of Table 2, Table 3 and § 5 that rest on § 3 and Appendix A are these. Each is checked against the statement that carries it.

**(a) "For the general linear group, an order with one step from a positive definite matrix that widens the aperture in some directions and leaves it unchanged in others admits only Gaussian families" (abstract, p. 1; p. 4, "every order of the scheme that contains one rank-deficient step admits only Gaussian families (Proposition 4.22(3))").** Carried by Theorem 3.7 (p. 21) through Proposition 4.22(3) (p. 49), whose proof uses Proposition 3.4(3) and Lemma 3.3(1) of this block. The chain is sound: any rank-deficient step $\Sigma \le_W \Sigma + P$ with $\Sigma$ definite is carried by $\Sigma^{-1/2}$ to $I \le_W I + \Sigma^{-1/2}P\Sigma^{-1/2}$, whose right end has least eigenvalue $1$, which is the hypothesis $wI \le_W wM$ of Proposition 4.22(3). Two qualifications are implicit in the headline and should be explicit: "order" means an order of the scheme (invariant under $\mathrm{GL}(d)$ and containing the rays), and "Gaussian families" means the restrictions to the pairs of $W$ of the affine Gaussian scale spaces (3.1), as Proposition 4.22(3) says. **Misleading only by omission.**

**(b) "The base must be positive definite: from $\Sigma = 0$ every step $0 \to P$ has the increment $\mu_P$" (p. 4).** The second half is true. The first half is **misleading**: positive definiteness of the base is what makes "$0 < \operatorname{rank} P < d$" the right hypothesis of Theorem 3.7, not what is needed for a singular step to force the Gaussian. Remark 3.13 itself (p. 25) forces the Gaussian from the step $I - e_1e_1^{\mathsf T} \le I$, whose base is singular. More generally, for the kernels (3.3) of Lemma 3.9 and a step $\Sigma \to \Sigma + P$ with $P$ singular, the exponent $-g(\langle\omega,(\Sigma+P)\omega\rangle) + g(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)$ vanishes on $\ker P$, so Lemma 3.8 applies whatever the base; with $\Sigma = e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}$ and $P = I - e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}$ (any $d \ge 2$) it gives $g(u+w) = g(u) + g(w)$ directly, Cauchy's equation, whose nonnegative solutions are linear. What fails for a singular base is the *form* of the hypothesis: with $\Sigma = e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}$ and $P = e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}$ the step is a ray and every self-decomposable kernel has an increment, although $0 < \operatorname{rank} P < d$. The sentence should say that the theorem is stated for a definite base because there "$P$ singular" is the right notion of a rank-deficient step, and that from the base $0$ nothing is forced. See required change 1.

**(c) "For $d \ge 2$ this pair admits the affine Gaussian scale spaces alone, on kernels and on operators (Theorems 3.6 and 3.12)" (p. 3); Table 2, cell $(\mathrm{GL}(d)$, Loewner$)$ (p. 32); § 5 (p. 54).** Carried exactly by Theorems 3.6 and 3.12. The cell is a complete description, and the text says so. **Correct.**

**(d) "The axioms make every aperture a linear image of the aperture at the identity, which is rotation invariant (Lemma 3.9)" (p. 4).** Carried by Lemma 3.9 and, more generally, by Proposition 3.4(3) for every order. **Correct.**

**(e) Table 2, cell $(\mathrm{GL}(d)$, rays$)$: "the orbits $(\Sigma^{1/2})_*\mu_I$ of the rotation-invariant self-decomposable laws $\mu_I \ne \delta_0$, one family for each law (Propositions 4.5 and 3.4(3))".** Carried by Proposition 3.4(3) (p. 18, proved pp. 76–77). Complete. **Correct.**

**(f) Table 2, cell (similarities, rays): "continuous covariant fields of symmetric self-decomposable laws over the shapes".** Carried by Proposition 3.4(2) with Proposition F.3. This is a characterization (every such field is a family and conversely), so the cell is described completely as a set; which fields exist over the shapes is problem 4 of § 5.1, and § 5 (p. 54) says so. But the set is larger than a reader of "aperture" expects: it contains fields with $\nu_\Sigma = \delta_0$ at positive definite $\Sigma$ (part 3, item (viii)). **Correct as stated; the text should say what the cell contains.**

**(g) "The axioms are stated on operators and on their kernels, and for every pair $(G,W)$ the two forms describe the same families (Lemmas A.1 and A.2)" (p. 5, "Scope"); "So each statement on the kernel families of a pair is one on its operator families."** Carried by Lemma A.2(2), which states the bijection, with Lemma A.1(4) as its inverse. **Correct.**

**(h) "Theorem 3.6 rests on other axioms. Its cascade is asked for only from a matrix to a larger one in the Loewner order, and its increments need not belong to the family. ... Neither earlier result implies it, and it implies neither of them (Remark 3.14)" (p. 5).** Consistent with Remark 3.14. One sentence of that remark, "It adds continuity in the pair" (p. 26, on Iijima), **understates** the theorem: see part 3, item (ii). The comparison with Iijima's axioms, which have no continuity, would be sharper if the text said that continuity is used only to pin the kernels at singular apertures.

**(i) "For the general linear group with the Loewner order Theorem 3.6 derives the additivity, and the Gaussian with it, from covariance and the cascade" (p. 6).** Correct: additivity $\mu_\Sigma * \mu_{\Sigma'} = \mu_{\Sigma+\Sigma'}$ is a consequence of (3.1).

**(j) § 1.1 (p. 6): "Every definition, lemma, proposition and theorem of §§ 3–4 and of the appendices has been formalized ... and its proof is machine-checked."** Consistent with Appendix I for this block, with the reservation of part 5.

**(k) "On the way it shows that the increments of a continuous cascade along every order with a path condition are infinitely divisible" (p. 6); § 3.3.** Carried by Theorem 3.5(2). Correct. The kernels from scale zero are infinitely divisible for *every* wedge datum, with or without (P), since the ray from $0$ is a $W$-chain for every $W$ of the scheme; the statement of Theorem 3.5(2) puts the kernels from scale zero under (P) unnecessarily (part 3, item (iii)).

**(l) "With the similarities, non-Gaussian families cascade along the Loewner order" (abstract, p. 1; p. 5, Proposition H.1(3)).** Not this block's claim, but this block already carries it: Proposition F.3(3)(ii), cited on p. 61 after Proposition A.3, gives the shape-blind family $\nu_\Sigma = ((\det\Sigma)^{1/(2d)}I)_*m$ along the Loewner order for every rotation-invariant self-decomposable $m$. The abstract's claim is therefore true by a trivial construction, and what Proposition H.1(3) adds is a family whose non-Gaussian part is not shape-blind. The abstract and § 1 should not let a reader think the survival of non-Gaussian families with the similarities is subtle; what is subtle is their shape, which Proposition 4.24(2) bounds. **Misleading by emphasis.**

Nothing in Table 3 or in the reading for imaging rests on this block beyond (a)–(d).

## 3. Mathematical correctness

I checked every proof of the block line by line. Findings, in the order of the text.

**(i) Definition 3.1 (p. 16).** Consistent. The ray order is a partial order on $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$ (antisymmetry at $\Sigma = 0$ included) and is invariant under every subgroup; the Loewner order likewise. The requirement that $G$ be closed is not used in any proof of this block (it is used for $\mathrm{S}(d)$ in Proposition F.3(1) and in the Lie-theoretic reading of § 4.4); harmless. The name "wedge datum ... after its order" (p. 16) is **misleading**: no wedge is part of the definition, Definition 3.1 "does not require one" (p. 16), and Theorem 4.3(3) exhibits an order of the scheme that comes from no cone.

**(ii) Lemma 3.9 and the role of (K2) in Theorem 3.6 (pp. 22–23, 65).** The proof is correct. Two remarks on what it uses.

First, the "No zeros" step of Lemma 3.9 (continuity along the ray of $I$ excludes a first zero) is a second route to a fact the paper already has: by the necessity half of Proposition 3.4(2) (p. 76), whose argument "reads only that $W$ contains the rays and $G$ the dilations", the kernels from scale zero of *every* $(G,W)$-family are self-decomposable, and by Proposition 3.4(1) a self-decomposable law has no zeros. So no $\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma$ of any family of the scheme has a zero, without continuity. The same first-zero argument is repeated in the proof of Proposition D.1(2) (p. 84). Consequently the hypothesis "if no $\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma$ has a zero" in Lemma 3.3(2) (p. 18) is always satisfied, and every family of the scheme is determined by its kernels from scale zero, with increments the quotients $\widehat{\mu}_{\Sigma'}/\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma$; the text states this only conditionally ("once their transforms have no zeros", pp. 17–18).

Second, the only place where (K2) is indispensable in the proof of Theorem 3.6 is the extension of (3.3) to singular $\Sigma$ ("Every kernel from scale zero", p. 65). Without (K2): $\mu_I$ is rotation invariant (K3), self-decomposable (K1 along the ray, K3 at the dilations), so $\widehat{\mu}_I = \vartheta(|\omega|^2)$ has no zero and $g = -\log\vartheta$ is continuous (a characteristic function is), nonnegative, $g(0) = 0$; (K3) gives $\widehat{\mu}_\Sigma = e^{-g(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle)}$ on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$; (K1) at $I \le I + e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}$ gives (3.2); Theorem 3.7 gives $g(u) = bu$; and the increment of every pair in $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ is the quotient. So **the Gaussian form of every kernel and every increment between positive definite apertures, and the "In particular" clause of Theorem 3.6, follow from (K1), (K3) and (K4) alone.** That (K2) is needed at the boundary is shown by the family
$$\mu_{\Sigma,\Sigma'} := N\big(0,\, 2b(\theta(\Sigma')\Sigma' - \theta(\Sigma)\Sigma)\big),\qquad \theta = 1 \text{ on } \mathrm{PD}(d),\ \theta = \theta_0 \in [0,1) \text{ on the singular matrices},$$
which satisfies (K1) (exponents add), (K3) ($\mathrm{GL}(d)$ preserves rank), (K4), has a positive semidefinite covariance on every Loewner pair ($\Sigma \le \Sigma'$ with $\Sigma'$ singular forces $\Sigma$ singular; with $\Sigma$ singular and $\Sigma'$ definite, $\Sigma' - \theta_0\Sigma \ge \Sigma' - \Sigma \ge 0$), agrees with (3.1) on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$, and is not (3.1) at the singular matrices; it fails (K2) along $(0,\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon,\dots,\varepsilon)) \to (0,\operatorname{diag}(1,0,\dots,0))$. (I checked the three claims numerically on random pairs; the algebra is one line each.) This is worth a sentence in § 3.4 and in Remark 3.14, since Iijima's axioms, as reported, have no continuity: on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ the hemigroup axioms need none either, and what replaces the differentiability of the transform at the origin that Felsberg and Sommer found implicit in Iijima's proof (p. 26) is positivity, through the sign of $g$ in Lemma 3.10. **Improvable; the printed hypotheses are not wrong.**

**(iii) Theorem 3.5(2) (p. 20).** Correct. The clause "In particular, for a wedge datum $(G,W)$ satisfying (P) every $\mu_{\Sigma,\Sigma'}$ ... is infinitely divisible, and so is every kernel from scale zero $\mu_\Sigma$" puts the second conclusion under (P) needlessly: $\zeta(s) = s\Sigma$ is a $W$-chain from $0$ to $\Sigma$ for every order containing the rays (the definition on p. 16 takes $0 \le c \le c'$), so the kernels from scale zero of every family of the scheme are infinitely divisible by clause (2) alone. The text notes this in passing for the stable orders (p. 21, "the ray chains from 0, which every order containing the rays has") but not in the statement. **Improvable.**

**(iv) Theorem 3.5(3)(c) (p. 20).** Correct: $\zeta(s)^{-1/2}\zeta(t)\zeta(s)^{-1/2} = QM^{t-s}Q^{\mathsf T}$ with $Q$ orthogonal is right, and $K_\alpha$ is closed under orthogonal conjugation and fractional powers. The partial-order property of $\le_{K_\alpha}$ is delegated to Proposition 4.7(2) (p. 65, "proved without this theorem"); that clause of Proposition 4.7 uses only the elementary equivalence (b)$\Leftrightarrow$(c) and the Loewner order, not Lemma 4.8 or C2, but Appendix I lists Proposition 4.7 as a whole under C2 (part 5).

**(v) Theorem 3.7 and Lemmas 3.8, 3.10 (pp. 21–23, 65–66).** Correct and complete. In the proof of Theorem 3.7 the equality of real exponentials gives the equality of exponents, and $(u,w)$ does range over $(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$ because $\dim H = d - r \ge 1$. In Lemma 3.10 the three steps (a constant difference on $[u_\delta,\infty)$, then everywhere by subdivision, then $h(\delta) = g(\delta) - K(\delta)$ invariant under halving and killed by continuity at $0$) are right, and monotonicity from $g \ge 0$ closes Cauchy's equation; I verified the index bookkeeping $n \ge \delta/((a-1)y)$. Lemma 3.8's proof (connectedness of $U$, density of $D$) is right; the alternative through Sasvári's product rule (p. 22) is also right.

**(vi) Theorem 3.6 and Theorem 3.12 (pp. 23–24).** Correct. The sufficiency half holds for every $d \ge 1$; the necessity needs $d \ge 2$ for $\operatorname{rank} e_1e_1^{\mathsf T} < d$. Theorem 3.12's uniqueness of $b$ through Lemma A.1(1) is right.

**(vii) Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.4 (pp. 18–19, 60, 76–77).** Correct. In Lemma 3.3(3) the (K3) check needs $(A\cdot\Sigma, A\cdot\Sigma')\in P_W$, which the $G$-invariance of $W$ gives; the proof (p. 60) does not say so, but it is the same point made in Lemma A.1(4). The "no zeros" proof of Proposition 3.4(1) (p. 76) is the standard argument with the inequality $1 - |\widehat\rho(2\omega)|^2 \le 4(1 - |\widehat\rho(\omega)|^2)$, correctly derived. Proposition 3.4(2) sufficiency uses self-decomposability at $c^{-1/2}$ and (b) at $A = c^{1/2}I$; right.

**(viii) Nondegeneracy at one pair (pp. 17, 61).** Not an error, but a consequence the text does not state. (K4) at $(0,I)$ propagates along the slice when $O(d)\subseteq G$ (Proposition A.3(1)) and to every definite aperture when $G = \mathrm{GL}(d)$ (Lemma A.5(5)). It does not propagate to every definite aperture for the similarities. Take $\phi:\Delta_d\to[0,1]$ continuous with $\phi(1/d,\dots,1/d) = 1$ and $\phi(\sigma_0) = 0$ at an interior point $\sigma_0$, and
$$\nu_\Sigma := N\big(0,\ \phi(\sigma(\Sigma))\,\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)\, I\big)\quad(\Sigma\ne 0),\qquad \nu_0 := \delta_0,$$
with $\sigma(\Sigma)$ the normalized spectrum of Proposition F.3. This map satisfies (a), (b) for every similarity ($\sigma$ and $\phi$ are invariant, $\operatorname{tr}$ and the Gaussian scale by $c$), (c) (the ordered eigenvalues are continuous, and $\nu_\Sigma\to\delta_0$ as $\Sigma\to0$), and every $\nu_\Sigma$ is self-decomposable; by Proposition 3.4(2) it is the family of kernels from scale zero of an $(\mathrm{S}(d),\text{rays})$-cascade family, a member of the cell of Table 2, with $\nu_{\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_0)} = \delta_0$ at a positive definite aperture and $\nu_I = N(0,dI)$. (Checked numerically as well.) So the scheme admits, for groups not transitive on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$, families in which a nondegenerate aperture measures nothing. The remark on p. 17 defends (K4) at one pair by the example of Proposition F.3(3)(ii), whose kernels vanish only at *singular* apertures, which is a different and more defensible thing. The author should either require $\mu_\Sigma\ne\delta_0$ for every $\Sigma\in\mathrm{PD}(d)$ (which keeps F.3(3)(ii), changes nothing for $\mathrm{GL}(d)$, and removes the example above from the similarity cells) or state in § 3.1 that the smaller-group cells contain such fields. **Improvable; the choice is the author's.**

**(ix) Lemma A.1 (pp. 56–58).** Correct: the interchange identity, the tightness through $\rho_1 * h_{\varepsilon_n}$ (a convergent sequence in $L^1$ has uniformly small tails; convolution with a unit-mass density on the unit ball moves mass by at most $1$), Prokhorov, the identification through $L^\infty$ functionals, and the strong uniqueness through $\widehat\varphi > 0$. Clause (2) (transport), (3), (4), (5) are right. The lemma is a known theorem (part 6).

**(x) Lemma A.2 (pp. 58–60).** Correct: the inversion formula for $\nu*\varphi_\sigma$, dominated convergence, Scheffé's lemma ($\|h_n - h\|_1 = 2\int(h-h_n)^+$), and the three-term split. Clauses (2) and (3) right.

**(xi) Proposition A.3 (pp. 61–62).** Correct. Clause (1) is right, including the derivation of (ND) at every pair of the slice from (K4) and the fact from [15, Lem. 6.3], which is proved inline. Clause (2) rests on [15, Prop. 6.6], restated "in the part used" (the existence of the gauge $\chi$ with $\chi(S_\lambda t) = \lambda\chi(t)$ from (A8) and (ND) of the marginal family): this restatement is load-bearing and is the only place in the block where a statement of the line paper is used in a proof; it is not reproved. Given it, the construction of $m$, of the $(\mathrm{GL}(d),\text{rays})$-family and the identification $\nu_{uI,vI} = \mu_{\theta(u),\theta(v)}$ by uniqueness of increments are right. Clause (3): $m$ is invariant under $((\varphi(t)\psi(t_0)/\psi(t))^{1/2}I)_*$ for every $t$, and $m\ne\delta_0$ by (ND), so the fact gives $\psi = \psi(t_0)\varphi$; right. Clause (4): the two families (i) and (ii) differ off the multiples of $I$ by Lemma A.5(2); right.

**(xii) Lemmas A.4, A.5 (pp. 62–64).** Correct. In A.5(1) the bound $c \le f(A/t) = \int\min(1,|y|/t)\,\mu(dy) \to 0$ uses $A_*\mu = \mu$ correctly, and closedness in $\mathrm{GL}(d)$ with $|\det|$ bounded away from $0$ gives compactness. A.5(2)'s polar-decomposition argument is right. A.5(3): the implications and the bijection are right; in "(c)$\Rightarrow$(a)" one needs $\mu_I\ne\delta_0$, which follows because $\mu_I$ lies in the orbit of a full law. A.5(5)'s diagonal example satisfies (K1)–(K4) as claimed.

**(xiii) Theorem 3.5 proof (pp. 64–65).** Correct: uniform continuity on the compact triangle, Lévy's continuity theorem (the limit $1$ is continuous), the portmanteau bound for the closed set $\{|x|\ge\varepsilon\}$, then C1 with $b_n = 0$. The hypotheses of C1 as stated in § 2.4 (null array, convergent row products) are met.

**(xiv) Remark 3.13 (p. 25).** The sketch is right, and the deduction "the first coordinate of $\mu_I$ is independent of the others, by rotation invariance every coordinate is" does yield mutual independence (apply $\widehat{\mu}_I(\omega) = \widehat{\mu}_I(\omega_1e_1)\widehat{\mu}_I(\omega - \omega_1e_1)$ coordinate by coordinate), which is what Kelker's lemma needs; the remark could say so, since one-against-the-rest independence is not in general mutual independence. **(xv) Remark 3.15 (p. 26).** Consistent with Proposition A.3 at $d = 1$; it leans on [16, Rem. 3.26] without restating it (part 7).

## 6. Novelty and relation to prior work, for this block

**Theorems 3.6 and 3.7 against Kelker, Iijima, Lindeberg and Pedersen–Sato.** The positioning in § 3.5 and § 1 is fair as far as I can check it. Kelker's lemma is the core of the rigidity, and Remark 3.13 says so; the single-step form (one step, any probability measure as increment, $e^{-g}$ not required to be a characteristic function) is, to my knowledge, new, and the proof through the sign of $g$ in Cauchy's equation is a genuine variant of Kelker's and of Cambanis–Huang–Simons' route. The comparison with Pedersen and Sato's cone-parameter semigroups (p. 6: additivity assumed, no covariance group, a Gaussian rigidity under moment hypotheses) is a correct contrast with hypotheses that neither contain nor are contained in those of Theorem 3.6. The report of Iijima's 1962–1963 axioms through Weickert, Ishikawa and Imiya (p. 241) I could not verify at the page (the host that carries the paper is outside this session's network); the paper's description (matrix scale, linearity, translation invariance, positivity, closure under invertible linear maps, composition into the family) is consistent with what is generally reported of that work, and Remark 3.14's conclusion that the two hypothesis sets are incomparable does not depend on details beyond that. The reference to Felsberg and Sommer on the implicit differentiability in Iijima's uniqueness proof ([20], p. 8 and n. 2, p. 24) is specific and plausible.

**Lemma A.1(1) is a known theorem, not credited.** That a bounded linear operator on $L^1(\mathbb R^d)$ commuting with translations is convolution with a bounded measure is Wendel's theorem (J. G. Wendel, *Left centralizers and isomorphisms of group algebras*, Pacific J. Math. 2 (1952), 251–261; in textbook form, Larsen, *An Introduction to the Theory of Multipliers*, Theorem 0.1.1). With positivity and mass preservation the measure is a probability measure. The paper proves it from scratch over a page and a half and cites only its own line paper ("the line's Lemma 2.2 proved again in $\mathbb R^d$ with balls for intervals", p. 17). **Required: cite the source; the proof may stay or be reduced to the positivity step.**

**Lemma A.5(1) is classical, not credited.** The compactness of the symmetry group $\{A : A_*\mu = \mu\}$ of a full law on $\mathbb R^d$ is a standard consequence of the convergence-of-types theorem (P. Billingsley, *Convergence of types in $k$-space*, Z. Wahrsch. 5 (1966), 175–179; it is stated in Jurek and Mason's § 2.3 on full measures and in Meerschaert and Scheffler's chapter 2, both books already cited by the paper). Lemma A.5(3)(b), that the orbit of a full law is indexed by $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ exactly when its stabilizer is conjugate to $O(d)$, is the "symmetry group conjugate to the orthogonal group" condition of Hudson–Mason and Holmes–Hudson–Mason, which the paper cites for another purpose on p. 41. **Required: cite; the lemma may stay for completeness.**

**Theorem 3.5(1).** Correctly credited to Sato (Thm. 9.7) on p. 20.

**Lemma 3.8.** Credited in substance to Sasvári (p. 22). Lemma 3.10 is an elementary variant of the monotone-solution theorem for Cauchy's equation; no citation is needed, but the text's claim that the sign replaces "every other regularity" (p. 24) could cite Aczél for the classical fact.

**Is it right that no prior work treats the order and the group of a linear family as a choice?** Within what I can check from this block: the closest objects are Urbanik's decomposability semigroup $\{A : \mu = A_*\mu * \rho\}$, which the paper identifies with the step set of an orbit (p. 41), and Pedersen–Sato's cone-parameter semigroups. Neither poses the order as a parameter of an axiom system with a group acting; I know of no scale-space paper that does. The claim on p. 5 is, as far as I can tell, right.

## 8. Required changes and suggestions (this block)

### Required changes

1. **p. 4, "The base must be positive definite: from $\Sigma = 0$ every step $0\to P$ has the increment $\mu_P$."** Reword: a singular nonzero base also forces the Gaussian (Remark 3.13 uses one); what the definite base buys is that "$P$ singular" is then the right hypothesis, and only the base $0$ forces nothing. As printed the sentence asserts a necessity that is false.
2. **pp. 56–58, Lemma A.1(1).** Cite Wendel (1952) (or Larsen, Thm. 0.1.1) for the representation of a translation-commuting bounded operator on $L^1$ as a convolution; the paper reproves a known theorem citing only its own line paper.
3. **pp. 63–64, Lemma A.5(1) and (3).** Cite the compactness of the symmetry group of a full law (Billingsley 1966; Jurek and Mason § 2.3 or Meerschaert and Scheffler ch. 2) and the "symmetry group conjugate to $O(d)$" condition (Hudson–Mason, Holmes–Hudson–Mason, already in the bibliography).
4. **pp. 6–7 and 103–104, Proposition A.3 in the trust base.** Say whether the formal proof of A.3 reproves [15, Prop. 6.6] (the gauge with $\chi(S_\lambda t) = \lambda\chi(t)$) or where else that statement enters; as printed, "machine-checked on Lean's logical axioms alone" and "proved in its released version" with "no formal development of the line paper" cannot all be read the same way.
5. **p. 16, "the last clause of the definition lists them as the corners."** Definition 3.1 has no such clause; the corners are in the paragraph that follows. Fix the reference.
6. **p. 22, Figure 1 caption.** Give the direction $u$ of $P = \tfrac32uu^{\mathsf T}$, without which the figure is not reproducible from the caption.

### Suggestions

1. **p. 18, Lemma 3.3(2), and p. 65, Lemma 3.9.** State once, after Proposition 3.4, that the kernels from scale zero of every $(G,W)$-family are self-decomposable and have no zeros, so every family is determined by them; then drop the conditional in Lemma 3.3(2), the first-zero argument in Lemma 3.9 and its repetition in Proposition D.1(2) (p. 84).
2. **pp. 21, 23, 26: the role of (K2).** Add that (K2) enters Theorem 3.6 only to pin the kernels at singular apertures; on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ the Gaussian form of every kernel and increment follows from (K1), (K3), (K4), and the family $N(0,2b(\theta(\Sigma')\Sigma' - \theta(\Sigma)\Sigma))$ with $\theta$ equal to $1$ on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$ and to $\theta_0<1$ on the singular matrices shows that (K2) is needed at the boundary. This sharpens Remark 3.14's "It adds continuity in the pair" against Iijima, whose axioms have no continuity.
3. **p. 20, Theorem 3.5(2).** State that every kernel from scale zero of every family of the scheme is infinitely divisible, with or without (P), by the ray chain from $0$.
4. **p. 17, nondegeneracy at one pair.** Either require $\mu_\Sigma\ne\delta_0$ on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$, or say that for the similarities the scheme admits fields such as $\nu_\Sigma = N(0,\phi(\sigma(\Sigma))\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)I)$ with $\nu = \delta_0$ at a positive definite shape; the defence on p. 17 through Proposition F.3(3)(ii) covers only kernels vanishing at singular apertures.
