## 1. Overall assessment and recommendation

**Recommendation: minor revision** (for this block; the other referees cover §§ 3, 4.4–4.5, 4.9 and the remaining appendices).

The mathematics of §§ 4.6–4.8 and of Appendices C and E is correct as far as I can check it, and I checked it line by line: Lemma 4.6, Proposition 4.7 with Lemma 4.8 and Remark 4.9, Propositions 4.10–4.13, Lemma 4.14, Propositions 4.15 and 4.17–4.20, Theorem 4.21 with the whole of Appendix C (Proposition 4.2, Lemma C.1, Theorem 4.3, Proposition 4.12, Proposition 3.4, Lemma 4.8, Propositions 4.13, 4.20 and 4.11(1), and the proof of Theorem 4.21 itself), Proposition 4.22, and Propositions E.1, E.2 and E.4 with Remark E.3. Every algebraic identity in this block that I recomputed (the stable step condition, the thresholds of Proposition 4.20, the Jacobi-formula computation behind Proposition 4.12, the subordination integrals of Proposition E.1, the constants $B_d$, $\Theta$ and $\varepsilon/(\pi(1+\varepsilon))$ of Proposition E.4, the numbers of § 4.8 and of Figure 2) came out as printed. I reproduced the grid computation of Remark E.5 from the description in the manuscript alone and obtained the six printed slopes to all four decimals.

What needs changing is small and definite. (i) The restated Lemma 2.9(2) on p. 12, which Proposition 4.19 rests on, states as "equivalently" a limit that is off by a factor of four ($2c\log a$ where the first half of the clause gives $\tfrac{c}{2}\log a$); the proof of Proposition 4.19 (p. 69) uses the correct value, so nothing downstream is affected, but a restated statement must not contradict itself. (ii) Two prose sentences (pp. 41) assert that the union of the exponent cones $\mathfrak k_\alpha$ is the Gaussian's cone $\{\operatorname{sym}B\ge0\}$; it is a proper subset, by the same reasoning the paper itself applies to the $C_\alpha$ in Theorem 4.21(1). (iii) Remark E.5 and open problem 6 conclude that at index 2 the lattice negative mass "follows the first-order term only for small steps" and that "the coefficient beyond first order is not known". The coefficient is computable by the method of Proposition E.4, and when the grid is compared with it the agreement at index 2 is as good as at index 1 (within 1–4% at all three $\varepsilon$); the reported discrepancy is an artefact of comparing with a first-order quantity. (iv) The novelty paragraph after Theorem 4.3 should be checked against Lawson's Ol'shanskii-decomposition work and Kostant's convexity theorem, which I believe already contain the "no closure" and majorization points for the Riemannian pair $(GL(d),O(d))$. The rest of my findings are improvable exposition.

## 2. The headline claims against what is proved (for this block)

**The speed limit (abstract, p. 1; § 1 p. 4; § 4.7; Table 3, p. 51).** The abstract says: for $0<\alpha,\gamma<2$ the linear images of the isotropic $\gamma$-stable law cascade along the order of index $\alpha$ exactly when $\gamma\ge\alpha$, and from $\alpha=2$ on only the Gaussian does. This is exactly Theorem 4.21(3)(c)–(d), and the proof (pp. 81–83) carries it. The chain claim "which allow faster changes of shape as $\alpha$ grows" is Theorem 4.21(1) with Proposition 4.7(3) and Lemma 4.1(3). The anisotropy bounds in Table 3, $(t_2/t_1)^{d}$ and $(t_2/t_1)^{d\alpha/(d+\alpha)}$, I recomputed from Proposition 4.7(3): $n_d/n_1\le n_1^{-d}\le (t_2/t_1)^{2d\alpha/(d+\alpha)}$, so $\sqrt{n_d/n_1}\le (t_2/t_1)^{d\alpha/(d+\alpha)}$. Correct. The numbers quoted in § 4.6 (p. 38: $2^{2/3}\approx1.59$ against 4 in the plane at $\alpha=1$) and in § 4.8 (p. 50: minor-axis factors 4, 2.52, 2.07 at $\alpha=1,1.5,1.9$ for slant $60^\circ$) are right; so is the "twice the volume scale" of the Figure 2 caption ($2^{3/2}/2^{1/2}$).

One qualification made in the body but not in the headline: Theorem 4.21 is a statement about *orders generated by invariant cones*. The abstract and § 1 (p. 4) speak of "a chain of orders"; that is accurate. But § 4.7's opening sentence, "the part kept decides how fast the shape may change", and Table 3's row "orbit of a self-decomposable profile" quietly carry the open converse of Proposition 4.13: for a general profile only the *ordered-density* steps are known to be steps, and Table 3 says "at least" and "not known in general" — correctly. § 5 (p. 54) says this too. I found no cell of Table 2 in this block whose text overstates what is proved: the $(GL(d),\le_\alpha)$ cell says "contains", not "is".

**"Completely in some cases".** For the general linear row the two complete cells are the rays (Propositions 4.5 and 3.4(3)) and the Loewner order (Theorem 3.6). The stable-order cell is complete *for the stable orbits* (Theorem 4.21(3)(c)) and a lower bound for general profiles. The table and § 5 say so cell by cell. Fine.

**The kernels one can compute cascade at most along rays (abstract; § 1 p. 4; Propositions 4.15, 4.17, 4.18, 4.19).** Proposition 4.15 is proved completely (pp. 68–69) and I checked the dilation-invariant-functional argument; Propositions 4.17 and 4.19 follow from it as stated; Proposition 4.18 follows once $\log(P/Q)$ is shown to satisfy the hypotheses, which the sketch does. Two scope remarks the headline should keep: the abstract's "zero-free decaying rational transform in $|\omega|^2$" is exactly Proposition 4.18's hypothesis ($\deg P>\deg Q$, neither vanishing on $[0,\infty)$, the quotient a characteristic function) — good; and the paragraph after Proposition 4.18 (p. 45) rightly says the converse fails (the tempered orbits of Proposition E.1(3) are not rational and keep the stable cone). The sentence in § 1 (p. 4), "These are the kernels computed by finitely many elliptic solves in the shape's operator", is therefore a sufficient, not a necessary, description; it reads as a characterization. Reword ("among them are …").

**Shape-adapted apertures are rank-deficient steps (abstract; § 1 p. 4; Proposition 4.22, p. 49).** Proposition 4.22(1)–(3) are proved completely in the text and are correct; clause (2) is Theorem 3.7 read on the orbit, which is what it says. The abstract's "Among linear images of rotation-invariant laws with a positive transform only the Gaussian reaches them by further smoothing" matches clause (2) exactly (the positivity of the transform is the hypothesis of Proposition 4.4 through $g\ge0$). The abstract's next sentence, "Every other such kernel measures the image afresh there, as the iteration of shape adaptation does anyway", is a reading, and § 4.8 and § 5 give it the right qualifications (the iteration convolves the original image each round; the fixed-volume normalization is a re-measurement for every family, Lemma 4.1(2)). I found no imaging sentence in §§ 4.6–4.8 that claims more than the theorem behind it.

**The "price" sentences (§ 4.6 p. 37, § 4.7 p. 47, § 4.8 pp. 50–51, § 5 p. 54).** "A non-Gaussian kernel pays in speed of shape change" is Theorem 4.21(3) read with Proposition 4.7(3); accurate for the stable orbits, and for other orbits it is a lower bound on the price (the step set of a general orbit is open, problem 2). § 4.8's "A stable orbit can follow the practice's shapes by moving up the scale axis to the boundary of its own step set" is Proposition 4.7(1)(c) at $\Sigma=wI$, which I verified.

**Figures 2 and 3 (pp. 30, 33).** The captions agree with the statements they cite (Lemma 4.1(4), Proposition 4.7(3), Theorem 4.21 in the plane: $k=\alpha/(\alpha+2)$, $\beta(C)=2k/(1-k)$, so $\beta(C)<2\iff k<\tfrac12$). I looked at the rendered pages; the drawn radii and slopes are consistent with the formulas.

### 3.2 Errors and gaps

1. **Lemma 2.9(2), p. 12 (restated from the isotropic paper; load-bearing for Proposition 4.19).** The clause reads: "$F_d(a\xi)-F_d(\xi)\to c\log a$ … Equivalently, for a unit vector $e$ the function $g(u):=F_d(\sqrt u\,e)$ satisfies $g(au)-g(u)\to2c\log a$." Since $g(au)=F_d(\sqrt a\sqrt u\,e)$, the first half gives $g(au)-g(u)\to c\log\sqrt a=\tfrac c2\log a$, not $2c\log a$. The Matérn law confirms it: $c=2\gamma$ (Proposition E.1(4)), $g(u)=\gamma\log(1+u)$, $g(au)-g(u)\to\gamma\log a=\tfrac c2\log a$. The proof of Proposition 4.19 (p. 69, "By Lemma 2.9(2), $g(au)-g(u)\to\tfrac12|S^{d-1}|k(0+)\log a$") and its statement ($\kappa=\tfrac12|S^{d-1}|k(0+)$) use the correct value, so no result is affected; but the restatement must be corrected, and the author should check which form the released isotropic paper states (if the error is there too, it needs an erratum there).

2. **Proposition 4.10, comment on p. 41, and "Prior art" on p. 41.** "The cone grows with $\alpha$, and its union over $\alpha<\infty$ is the Gaussian's cone $\{\operatorname{sym}B\ge0\}$" and "for the standard Gaussian it is $\{\operatorname{sym}B\ge0\}$, the union of the $\mathfrak k_\alpha$" are both wrong as written. A $B$ with $\operatorname{sym}B\ge0$, $\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym}B)=0$ and $B$ not skew ($B=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ in the plane) satisfies $(d+\alpha)\cdot0\ge\operatorname{tr}B>0$ for no $\alpha$, yet it is an exponent of $N(0,I)$ (the increment is $N(0,\operatorname{diag}(1-e^{-2t},0))$). The union is $\{\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym}B)>0\}\cup\{\text{skew}\}$; its closure is the Gaussian's cone. This is the same boundary phenomenon the paper states carefully for the $C_\alpha$ in Theorem 4.21(1) (p. 47–48), and it matters there: the nonzero boundary of $C_2$ carries no non-Gaussian family. Fix both sentences.

3. **Remark E.5 (p. 88) and open problem 6 (pp. 55–56): a wrong conclusion drawn from a computation.** See § 4 below. The index-2 slopes do *not* "follow the first-order term only for small $\varepsilon$": they follow the exact continuum rate at every $\varepsilon$ computed, and that rate is computable. The sentence "the coefficient beyond first order is not known" should go.

4. **Proposition 4.19, commentary p. 46.** "every subordinated isotropic datum (Definition 2.8) whose delay profile is nonzero and bounded at the origin" belongs to the class only when the datum has Gaussian coefficient $a=0$ (the proposition is about the orbit of Proposition 4.13(1), which has no Gaussian part; a subordinated datum with $b_0>0$ has one). Add "with $a=0$".

5. **Proposition 4.13's title and the word "cone" (pp. 43–44, 47, Table 3).** The set of steps $\{M:\lambda_{\min}(M)\ge1,\ \det M\le\lambda_{\min}(M)^dR(\lambda_{\min}(M)^{1/2})^2\}$ is called "the ordered-density cone" throughout, and § 4.6 (p. 38) says the dilation ratio "gives a cone of steps". It is a step set and, by clause (3), a partial order, but it is not shown to be $\exp C$ for an invariant cone $C$: closure under $M\mapsto M^\tau$ would need $R(\mu^{1/\tau})^\tau\le R(\mu)$, and supermultiplicativity gives the opposite inequality. Open problem 2 (p. 55) acknowledges that the step set of a general orbit may not come from a cone. Either prove closure under fractional powers for this set (I do not see it) or rename it "the ordered-density step set" and say in one sentence why "cone" is not claimed.

6. **Minor imprecisions in Appendix C that do not affect correctness.** (a) Proof of Proposition 4.10 (p. 67): the relative step of $(\Sigma_t,I)$ is $\Sigma_t^{-1}=e^{tB^{\mathsf T}}e^{tB}$, not $e^{tB}e^{tB^{\mathsf T}}$; same eigenvalues, so the argument stands. (b) Proposition 4.12(2) and its proof (p. 76) identify $\mathfrak k_\alpha$ with $\{B:e^{tB}e^{tB^{\mathsf T}}\in\exp C_\alpha\}$ through Proposition 4.10, which was stated for $e^{tB^{\mathsf T}}e^{tB}$; add the one clause that the two products are isospectral. (c) Proof of Lemma 4.8, p. 77: the bound $|e^{-s}-1+s|\le s^2$ "for $|s|\le1$" is used for $s=E(\zeta/R)\ge0$ small; $|e^{-s}-1+s|\le s^2/2$ for all $s\ge0$ avoids the case split.

All six printed slopes are reproduced to four decimals, so the remark is described well enough to be reproduced, with two omissions to repair: the base of the logarithm (natural; against $\log_2$ or $\log_{10}$ the slopes are $0.0737$ and $0.2449$ at $\gamma=1,\varepsilon=0.5$) and the fitting method (ordinary least squares on the four points). The total mass is $1$ to machine precision and the imaginary part of the inverse transform is below $10^{-17}$, as it should be for an even real symbol.

**Is the check well designed?** Yes, within its purpose. The per-doubling increments are nearly constant ($0.0029,0.0031,0.0031$; $0.1062,0.1068,0.1059$), which is what "linear in $\log t$" means on four points; the fit residuals are below $10^{-3}$. The box width is immaterial: $n=32t$ and $n=128t$ give the same masses to five decimals (the index-1 kernel decays like $e^{-|x|/t}$, so wrap-around is negligible at $32t$). A rotated step ($45^\circ$, which exercises the $b_\pm$ terms of the eight-neighbour symbol) gives slope $0.1092$ at $\gamma=1,\varepsilon=0.5$ against $0.1061$: the stencil's anisotropy at the grid scale shows at the $3\%$ level, and the remark might say that the diagonal steps it reports are the ones the five-point stencil treats isotropically.

**The index-2 conclusion is wrong.** Remark E.5 compares the index-2 slopes with "$\gamma$ times the index-1 values, the first-order term in $P$", finds $0.1768$ against $0.2122$ at $\varepsilon=0.5$, and concludes that the grid "follow[s] it only for small $\varepsilon$"; open problem 6 says "the coefficient beyond first order is not known". But the coefficient is given by the same mechanism as Proposition E.4, at every index: the would-be increment's transform tends, along rays, to the degree-0 homogeneous function $h(\omega)=(\langle\omega,\Sigma\omega\rangle/\langle\omega,\Sigma'\omega\rangle)^\gamma$ (this is the $h$ of Proposition 4.15 and Remark 4.16), and the leading $|x|^{-d}$ singularity of the increment at the origin is the inverse transform of $h$ minus its mean, whose angular part in the plane is $A(\theta)=\sum_{m\ge1}a_m(-1)^m\frac m\pi\cos2m\theta$ when $h=a_0+\sum_m a_m\cos2m\varphi$ (the inverse transform of $\cos2m\varphi$ in $\mathbb R^2$ is $(-1)^m\frac m\pi\cos2m\theta/|x|^2$). The log-rate of the negative mass is $\int_0^{2\pi}A_-\,d\theta$. At $\gamma=1$ this reproduces $\varepsilon/(\pi(1+\varepsilon))$ exactly (last column, rows 1–3: a check of the method against Proposition E.4(4)). At $\gamma=2$ it gives $0.00627$, $0.05526$, $0.17771$, and the grid slopes $0.0060$, $0.0544$, $0.1768$ are within $1$–$4\%$ of them at every $\varepsilon$ — the same agreement as at index 1. So the grid follows the continuum rate at index 2 for all three steps; what it does not follow is a first-order proxy that is simply the wrong comparison at $\varepsilon=0.5$. For a proof at index 2 one writes $\hat\rho=1-2\langle\omega,P\omega\rangle\hat K_1'+\langle\omega,P\omega\rangle^2\hat K_2'$ with $K_2'$ the index-2 Matérn density and expands as in Remark E.3; the fourth derivatives of $K_2'\sim c|y|^{4-d}$ (or $|y|^2\log|y|$ at $d=2$) give the $|y|^{-d}$ term. Required change: replace the comparison column at $\gamma=2$ by the exact coefficient (or at least by the value above), delete "they follow it only for small $\varepsilon$", and rewrite open problem 6 accordingly (what remains open there is the lattice law itself, which is fairly stated as a numerical finding).

**Is anything concluded that a computation cannot show?** No: the remark says "a numerical finding and is not proved", § 1.1 says the statements are independent of the computed values, and Proposition E.4 carries the continuum statement. Figures 2 and 3 are drawn from formulas that I verified against the statements; the caption of Figure 2 says the discs were checked against the orders on random steps, which is the right kind of self-test to mention.

**Remark H.3** is outside my block; I did not reproduce it.

## 6. Novelty and relation to prior work (this block)

- **Lemma 4.6, Proposition 4.7, Proposition 4.10 (pp. 38–42).** The Lévy measure of the elliptical stable law is classical, as the text says. The step condition $\lambda_{\min}(M)^{d+\alpha}\ge\det M$ is a two-line consequence of ordering two such densities; I could not find it computed in the literature on decomposability semigroups (Urbanik 1972, Jurek–Mason 1993) and the hedged claim "new against the sources read" (p. 42) is acceptable. The identification of $\mathfrak k_\alpha$ with the symmetric-part condition is likewise elementary once Jurek–Mason's Prop. 3.4.1 is applied; the paper says so.
- **Proposition 4.11 (anisotropy budget).** I know of no prior bound $\operatorname{tr}B<(d+2)\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{sym}B)$ for rotation-invariant non-Gaussian operator-self-decomposable laws; the sharpness through $S_\alpha(I)$ is new as far as I can tell.
- **Theorem 4.3 and its "Prior art" (p. 36).** The paper claims as new "the equality of the order with $\exp C$ without a closure" and "the proof by majorization". For the Riemannian symmetric pair $(GL(d),O(d))$ with $\mathfrak p=\mathrm{Sym}(d)$, the statement that $O(d)\exp C$ is a closed subsemigroup for every $O(d)$-invariant closed convex cone $C\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(d)$ — which is exactly Lemma C.1(5) plus Proposition 4.2(3) — is, to my knowledge, part of Lawson's work on Ol'shanskii decompositions (J. D. Lawson, *Polar and Ol'shanskii decompositions*, J. reine angew. Math. 448 (1994) 191–219; see also Hilgert–Neeb, *Lie semigroups and their applications*, 1993, and Neeb, *Holomorphy and convexity in Lie theory*, 2000, Thm. XI.1.7), and the majorization input is Kostant's convexity theorem (*Ann. Sci. ENS* 1973), of which Gel'fand–Naimark is the $GL(d)$ case. I have not been able to read those pages for this review, so I state it as something the author must check: if the Riemannian case with $K$ compact is covered there, the novelty claim should shrink to the step-set dictionary on $\mathrm{PD}(d)$, the characterization by fractional powers, the non-pointed case, and the elementary self-contained proof. The remark that Hilgert–Hofmann–Lawson's divisibility theorem is for connected groups while $GL(d)$ is not is fair, but the issue disappears on passing to $GL^+(d)$ or $SL(d)$, which the paper's own Proposition D.1 invites; say why that reduction was not taken.
- **Proposition 4.12(3)** honestly says the stable orders are the Loewner order in other coordinates; good.
- **Proposition E.1(3)** correctly places the two families in Rosiński's tempered stable class and Ryznar's relativistic stable class; the definitions quoted (completely monotone tempering, proper when $q(0+,u)=1$, exponent $(m^{2/\alpha}+|\omega|^2)^{\alpha/2}-m$) agree with those sources as far as I could verify from secondary accounts. The observation that the relativistic law is *not* tempered $\alpha$-stable in Rosiński's sense (p. 85–86), with the tempering sitting in the subordinator, is a nice point I have not seen stated.
- **The Matérn covariance** (Proposition 4.4(4), 4.17, E.4) is correctly identified with the geometrically anisotropic Matérn of Lindgren–Rue–Lindström for $\gamma>d/2$; the statement that no change of the shape matrix other than a rescaling is a further smoothing is new to me and is proved.
- **Cuppens' criterion (p. 43)** and the Linnik–Ostrovskii reference: I could not verify the theorem numbers; the claim (stable and Matérn laws have non-infinitely-divisible factors) is classical for the stable laws on the line, and the multivariate statement with a positive continuous Lévy density is plausible. The sentence does no work in any proof; keep it only if the page is verified.

## 7. Exposition and structure (this block)

- **§ 4.6 is the longest subsection of the paper (pp. 37–47) and carries four sub-headings, each a different object** (stable rung, dilation ratio, index 0, the order on $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$). It reads well sentence by sentence but a reader loses the thread between Lemma 4.8 and Proposition 4.20. One paragraph at the head of § 4.6 listing the four questions and their answers in order (what the stable orbit survives; what any profile survives at least; which orbits survive nothing; how the stable order extends to $\mathrm{PSD}(d)$) would help, and the "rung" vocabulary (introduced p. 38, used in Lemma 4.14(4) and the title of § 4.8) is used too rarely to earn a definition.
- **Definition used before it is given.** "exponent spread", "eccentricity" and "shape-blind" are defined in the Table 2 glossary (p. 31) and again in Proposition 4.24 (p. 53); § 4.6 does not need them. "$k_d$ versus $k$" (polar profile against the profile against $r^{-d}dx$) is explained on p. 42 and in the letter list on p. 14; the factor $|S^{d-1}|$ is a trap (it is the source of the $\tfrac12|S^{d-1}|$ in Proposition 4.19 and of the Lemma 2.9 slip above). A displayed line "$k=k_d/|S^{d-1}|$; $\kappa=\tfrac12 k_d(0+)$" near Proposition 4.19 would prevent confusion.
- **Forward references that are load-bearing.** Proposition 4.7 relies on Lemma 4.8, proved in Appendix C; Theorem 4.21 is proved entirely in Appendix C. That is a defensible layout since Appendix C is self-contained and in dependence order, but the proof of Proposition 4.7(1) in § 4.6 cites "Lemma 4.8" for (a)$\Rightarrow$(b) and then Remark 4.9 gives a second route in six lines that is complete; consider making Remark 4.9 the proof in the body and leaving Lemma 4.8 for Proposition 4.24(3), which is the only place the Gaussian factor $C$ is needed.
- **Leaning on earlier papers.** In this block only Lemma 2.9 is used (Proposition 4.19), and it is restated; the restatement is where the error is. Prop E.1(4) re-derives the Matérn profile rather than citing the isotropic paper; good.
- **Appendix C** is well organized and genuinely checkable; the opening list (p. 70) saying what is proved where is the model the other appendices should follow. The orders paragraph of the proof of Theorem 4.21 (p. 81) would be easier to read as a lemma ("Lemma C.2: for $C$ as in Theorem 4.3(4), $\preceq_C^+$ is a $GL(d)$-invariant partial order with the path condition, proved from the causal curves alone").
- **Appendix E.** Proposition E.2 duplicates Proposition 4.17 at index 1 with the increment named; it could be a remark. Remark E.3, Proposition E.4 and Remark E.5 belong together and are, as a journal version, the natural place to keep the only computation.
- **What a journal version should move or drop in this block.** Keep Lemma 4.6–Proposition 4.13, Proposition 4.15, 4.17, 4.19, 4.20, Theorem 4.21 and Proposition 4.22 in the body. Move Lemma 4.14(5) (the dyadic and $\beta=2$ examples), Remark 4.16 (the second route to the analytic step), Proposition 4.18's list of examples, and the "Profiles stable at the origin" paragraph to the appendix or a remark. Drop the Cuppens sentence. Appendix C should stay (it is the proof of the main theorem); Appendix E.1 could be shortened to clause (1) and the two families, with the classification against Rosiński and Ryznar in one paragraph.
